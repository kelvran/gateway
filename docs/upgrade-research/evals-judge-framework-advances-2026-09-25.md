# Kelvran Evals — Judge-Panel, Mixture-SPRT & Eval-Framework Advances

**Date:** 2026-09-25
**Scope:** `evals/` — the Python agent-evaluation deployable, specifically its LLM-judge panel (2-judge, same-vendor Bedrock Claude Sonnet 5 + Haiku 4.5), its mixture-SPRT (anytime-valid) early-stopping rule, `--judge-debias` bias-consistency checking, and its Docker-sandboxed rollout execution model. Excludes the Go `gateway/` and embedded Cache — see the sibling reports in this directory.
**Method:** Adversarial multi-source research (3-vote verification per claim) against real, currently-shipping/published 2026 LLM-judge and anytime-valid-testing practice: Inspect AI (UK AISI), AlpacaEval, Prometheus 2, DeepEval, Braintrust (via a shared third-party "TypeSafe/Jev" integration), the "Trust or Escalate" cascaded-judge paper, and two 2026 e-value statistical-methodology papers (Sokolova & Sokolov; Brannath & Fischer). Every candidate finding was first checked against `evals/ARCHITECTURE.md`, `PRD.md`'s Evals section, `DECISIONS.md`'s judge-panel/early-stopping entries, and (where directly relevant to bias claims) `THREAT_MODEL.md`'s judge Tampering row, so this report only surfaces what is genuinely new relative to Kelvran's own disclosed, reasoned positions — not a restatement of the same-vendor-panel tradeoff, mSPRT's own Johari/Koomen/Pekelis/Walsh (KDD 2017) construction, or the Docker→gVisor→Firecracker sandbox-tiering plan, all of which are already documented and accepted there. 25 claims were put to adversarial vote across 16 distinct primary sources; 10 confirmed, synthesized below into 7 findings after merging claims that describe the same underlying paper or product; 15 refuted (see Caveats).

## Executive summary

Seven genuinely new developments bear on `evals`' judge panel, mixture-SPRT early-stopping, `--judge-debias`, and sandboxed rollout model — none of them restating the same-vendor-panel tradeoff, mSPRT's own construction, or the sandbox-tiering plan Kelvran's docs already own. The most directly actionable: Inspect AI ships a structural delimiter-sanitization step in its judge-prompt construction that neutralizes exactly the adaptive judge-prompt-injection threat `THREAT_MODEL.md` already discloses `--judge-debias` does **not** defend against — a concrete, low-effort mitigation for a gap Kelvran has named but not yet closed. Two 2026 statistical-methodology papers show mixture-SPRT-family e-value tests are provably conservative, and that their power disadvantage versus a calibrated group-sequential design shrinks — and can reverse — specifically under continuous, every-single-trial monitoring, which is exactly the cadence Kelvran's own mSPRT already uses; neither paper is discussed anywhere in `ARCHITECTURE.md` or `DECISIONS.md`. AlpacaEval's Length-Controlled win-rate exposes a length/verbosity bias dimension that `--judge-debias`'s position-swap mechanism does not structurally catch, and "Trust or Escalate" offers a cascaded, confidence-gated judge-escalation design carrying a provable, user-configurable human-agreement guarantee — a different shape from both Kelvran's flat 2-judge panel and the "pairwise vs. independent-refutation" skeptic-panel question `PRD.md` still leaves open. Separately, Prometheus 2 demonstrates a self-hostable, weight-merged open-weight judge model as a genuinely non-Bedrock, non-Claude alternative judge architecture, and a September-2026 "typed-probability" non-generative verdict layer (a third-party "Jev" model) shipped inside both DeepEval and Braintrust as a structurally different fix for judge verdict-flip/parsing variance than a multi-judge panel. No genuinely new finding survived on the sandboxed rollout execution model itself — the two candidate claims that targeted it (Inspect AI's sandbox-restore hardening; a strict-majority panel-tie-handling rule) were both refuted on verification.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. Structural delimiter sanitization in the judge prompt — a concrete mitigation for a gap Kelvran already discloses is open (Small)

**What:** Inspect AI's `model_graded_qa()` scorer rewrites any `[BEGIN DATA]`/`[END DATA]` structural delimiter markers that appear inside model-controlled text (the candidate `answer`, the question, and the criterion) before that text is inserted into the judge-grading prompt — so a candidate response cannot inject a fake delimiter and smuggle its own grading instructions into the judge's context.

**Why it matters for Kelvran specifically:** `THREAT_MODEL.md`'s Judge Tampering row already discloses, in its own words, that `--judge-debias` is an opt-in bias-**consistency** check, not a defense against adaptive judge prompt-injection embedded in the candidate output — citing JudgeDeceiver's 79–93% survival rate against position-swap debiasing specifically. This finding is a concrete, low-effort mechanism that targets that exact named gap, one `evals` does not currently implement anywhere in `judge/llm_judge.py`'s prompt construction.

**Consistent with settled decisions?** Additive, not a revisit — `THREAT_MODEL.md` names CoT-forcing, reference-guided grading, judge-model≠policy-model, and (bias-consistency only) `--judge-debias` as the row's current defenses, and explicitly leaves the adaptive-injection gap open rather than claiming it's covered. No RFC or decision anywhere rejects or defers delimiter sanitization; it has simply never been raised.

**2026 best practice grounding:**
- Inspect AI's own docs state plainly under a "Grading Robustness" heading: "Inspect rewrites any `[BEGIN DATA]` / `[END DATA]` markers that appear in the model-controlled `answer`... so a model cannot inject a fake delimiter and smuggle grading instructions into the prompt" [inspect.aisi.org.uk — confirmed 3-0].
- This is backed by real, shipped code, not aspirational docs: the upstream source (`inspect_ai/scorer/_model.py`) contains an actual `_STRUCTURAL_DELIMITER_RE` regex and a `neutralize_structural_delimiters()` function applied to the completion, question, and criterion before prompt construction, with an explicit code comment naming the exact threat: "so a model cannot inject fake `[END DATA]` / `[BEGIN DATA]` markers into the judge prompt" [github.com/UKGovernmentBEIS/inspect_ai — confirmed 3-0].

**Concrete next step:** add a delimiter-neutralization pass (rewriting any occurrence of the judge prompt template's own structural markers found inside the candidate output/reference/criterion strings, e.g. to a visually distinct escaped form) in `evals/judge/llm_judge.py`'s prompt-building path, applied uniformly whether or not `--judge-debias`, `--judge-axes`, or `--llm-judge-panel` is active — this is a property of the prompt template, not any one judging mode.

**Effort:** Small — one sanitization helper applied at the single call site that assembles the judge prompt string, no new dependency, no change to `Score`'s shape.

### 2. Kelvran's mixture-SPRT is already in the power-favorable monitoring regime — worth confirming and documenting, not redesigning (Small–Medium)

**What:** A 2026 statistical-methodology paper (Sokolova & Sokolov, *E-values for Adaptive Clinical Trials*) shows, via reproducible simulation, that the power gap between a calibrated group-sequential (GS) design and an e-value/anytime-valid rule is not fixed — it depends on the monitoring schedule, and the gap shrinks, and can reverse, as monitoring moves from sparse pre-planned checkpoints toward continuous (every-new-data-point) monitoring.

**Why it matters for Kelvran specifically:** `docs/rfcs/2026-09-05-evals-mixture-sprt-early-stopping.md` already checks the running Wilson-style likelihood ratio "after every single trial (not two pre-declared checkpoints), with no fixed checkpoints and no Bonferroni correction" — i.e., Kelvran's mSPRT already runs at the continuous-monitoring end of the schedule spectrum this paper studies, which per its own numerical results (Table 5 of the full paper: a recalibrated fixed-schedule boundary collapses to 10.0% power under continuous monitoring while the e-value rule reaches 75.0%) is exactly the regime where the power case against mSPRT-style anytime-valid testing is weakest. This is a genuinely new, quantified confirmation that Kelvran's existing design choice (continuous checks, not fixed checkpoints) was the power-favorable one — a property the mSPRT RFC asserted qualitatively (no Bonferroni correction needed) but never benchmarked against a calibrated fixed-schedule alternative under varying monitoring cadence.

**Consistent with settled decisions?** Additive confirmation, not a challenge — neither `evals/ARCHITECTURE.md` nor `DECISIONS.md` discuss group-sequential/alpha-spending designs at all; the mSPRT RFC's own "Alternatives Considered" section compares only against Howard/Ramdas/McAuliffe/Sekhon's nonparametric time-uniform confidence sequences, never against a calibrated GS design under varying monitoring frequency. Worth flagging: a separate, narrower sub-claim from the same paper — that a calibrated GS rule has the *highest* power specifically under the paper's own sparse **baseline** schedule — was itself refuted on verification (1-2 vote) for overreach; the survived, high-confidence claim is specifically about the *schedule-dependent reversal*, not a blanket "GS is always better except at continuous monitoring" ranking.

**2026 best practice grounding:**
- "Extended simulations show that the power gap between group sequential and e-value methods depends on the monitoring schedule and reverses under continuous monitoring" — verbatim from the paper's own abstract [arxiv.org/abs/2602.06379 — confirmed 3-0], with the specific numbers (87.0%/68.6% at 5 looks; 86.1%/72.3% at 20 looks; 10.0%/75.0% under continuous monitoring) drawn directly from the paper's Section 10.5 and Table 5.

**Concrete next step:** no code change needed — this is a documentation/confirmation item. Add a short note to `evals/ARCHITECTURE.md`'s mSPRT section (or the RFC itself) citing this result as external validation that the "check after every trial, no fixed checkpoints" design is in the power-favorable regime, closing the RFC's own previously-unbenchmarked assumption with a concrete citation rather than leaving it as an internal qualitative claim.

**Effort:** Small–Medium — a documentation update; optionally, a follow-on simulation against Kelvran's own regression-corpus pass/fail rates to confirm the same ordering holds for Kelvran's specific Bernoulli parameters (not required to capture the finding, but would make it fully self-verified rather than borrowed).

### 3. AlpacaEval's Length-Controlled win-rate — a bias dimension `--judge-debias` does not structurally catch (Medium)

**What:** AlpacaEval's Length-Controlled (LC) win-rate fits a logistic regression to predict the judge's preference from (instruction, model, length-difference), then computes a counterfactual "what would the preference be at equal length" — a debiasing technique targeting verbosity/length bias specifically, distinct from position bias. It reduces length-gameability roughly 3× (21% → 6%) and raises Spearman correlation with Chatbot Arena from 0.93 to 0.98.

**Why it matters for Kelvran specifically:** `--judge-debias` (`docs/rfcs/2026-09-09-evals-judge-debiasing-position-swap.md`) makes each judge call twice — reference-first, then candidate-first — and fails closed on **disagreement** between the two swapped calls. That mechanism is well-suited to catching order-dependent (position) bias, but a judge that prefers the longer response regardless of which position it occupies would still agree with itself across the swap, producing no disagreement and no fail-closed trigger — i.e., a purely order-independent length preference can evade the disagreement-based check by construction, not just by omission. Worth noting precisely: `THREAT_MODEL.md`'s own Judge Tampering row names `--judge-debias` as catching "position/verbosity/self-preference" bias together, but the mechanism itself (swap-and-compare-for-disagreement) is specifically a position-bias detector; whether it actually catches an order-independent verbosity preference is a mechanism-level question that row's prose does not resolve, and AlpacaEval's LC methodology is grounded in solving exactly that gap with a distinct (regression-based counterfactual, not swap-and-compare) technique.

**Consistent with settled decisions?** Genuinely new — `ARCHITECTURE.md`, `PRD.md`, and `DECISIONS.md` contain zero mentions of length bias or verbosity bias as a distinct dimension from position bias; `DECISIONS.md`'s 2026-09-09 entry describes `--judge-debias` purely as a position-swap mechanism with no length/verbosity-specific logic.

**2026 best practice grounding:**
- "The main idea is that for each model we will fit a logistic regression to predict the preference of the autoannotator given: (1) the instruction, (2) the model, and (3) the difference of length... we can then try to predict the counterfactual" [github.com/tatsu-lab/alpaca_eval — confirmed 2-1, primary official repo].
- "the relative length gameability was ~21% for AlpacaEval and decreases to ~6% for LCAE" and correlation with Chatbot Arena rises "from 0.93 to 0.98 Spearman correlation" — both confirmed verbatim against the same repo, cross-checked against the companion arXiv paper (Dubois et al., 2404.04475).
- The repo is explicit about scope: "we are only controlling for length bias. There are other known biases that we are not controlling for" — confirming this is a distinct, narrowly-scoped technique, not a general debiasing suite.

**Concrete next step:** add an opt-in length/verbosity-consistency check as a sibling to `--judge-debias` — either a length-counterfactual re-scoring pass (AlpacaEval's own regression approach, which needs a corpus of scored (output, length) pairs to fit against) or a simpler length-swap variant (re-score after truncating/padding the longer response to match the shorter one's length, fail-closed on disagreement, mirroring the existing position-swap pattern's operational shape).

**Effort:** Medium — a new, bounded scoring mode in `evals/judge/`, following `--judge-debias`'s own precedent of orchestration living outside `judge()`'s core.

### 4. A typed-probability, non-generative verdict layer — a structurally different fix for judge verdict-flip variance than a panel (Medium)

**What:** A third-party model called "Jev" (from TypeSafe AI) now plugs into both DeepEval and Braintrust as a drop-in replacement for the free-text "verdict" generation step inside an LLM-judge call. Instead of asking the judge model to write yes/no/borderline prose and parsing it, Jev returns calibrated typed probabilities directly — three primitives (`Noul` for yes/no, `Choice` for closed-set labels, `Score` for ordered scales) — mapped through fixed thresholds, with no free-form generation and no JSON-recovery step.

**Why it matters for Kelvran specifically:** Kelvran's own multi-judge panel (`evals.judge.llm_judge.reduce_panel_votes`) exists to address correlated/inconsistent judge verdicts via majority-vote-with-fail-closed-tie, and `Score.quorum_reached` already tracks exactly this class of problem at the panel-aggregation level. Jev is a different layer entirely — it targets the *verdict-generation* step itself, before any panel-reduction logic runs, replacing free-text-parsing variance with a structurally-typed decision. This is not a repeat of the panel-composition tradeoff `DECISIONS.md` already accepted; it's an orthogonal, potentially complementary mechanism (a panel of Jev-backed judges would still need aggregation, but each individual verdict would no longer carry parsing/verdict-flip variance of its own).

**Consistent with settled decisions?** Genuinely new — nothing in `ARCHITECTURE.md`, `PRD.md`, or `DECISIONS.md` discusses a non-generative or typed-probability alternative to free-text judge verdicts; the existing panel-reduction logic assumes each judge call returns a parseable pass/fail/score from generated text.

**2026 best practice grounding:**
- DeepEval's own release notes (v4.2.4, dated 2026-09-22, 3 days before this research date): "Jev returns typed probabilities instead, which DeepEval maps through fixed thresholds without free-form generation or JSON recovery," explicitly framed as addressing "verdict variance" [deepeval.com/blog/introducing-jev-in-deepeval — confirmed 3-0], independently corroborated by TypeSafe's own docs (`docs.typesafe.ai`, naming the same three primitives) and by DeepEval's product docs (`deepeval.com/docs/metrics-introduction`, a real `deepeval set-typesafe` CLI command and `llm`/`hybrid`/`system_one` eval modes) and GitHub release/PR history (python-v4.2.4, PR #3332).
- Braintrust shipped the same underlying "TypeSafe" feature the same month: "You can also use Jev as the judge in a scorer or classifier... Braintrust records the confidence and per-choice probabilities behind each decision" [braintrust.dev/docs/changelog — confirmed 2-1, corroborated by `braintrust.dev/docs/integrations/ai-providers/typesafe`].

**Concrete next step:** evaluate Jev/TypeSafe as an optional, pluggable verdict backend in `evals/judge/providers.py` (a new `make_typesafe_call_model()`-shaped provider, sibling to the existing Anthropic/OpenAI/Bedrock provider functions) and pilot it against `tests/fixtures/regression_corpus_judge_accuracy.json` to see whether it reduces verdict-flip variance on Kelvran's own 24-case judge-accuracy corpus before committing to it as a default.

**Effort:** Medium — a new third-party dependency (`typesafe-sdk` + API key) and provider function; no change to `Score`'s shape or panel-reduction logic required for a pilot.

### 5. Cascaded, confidence-gated judge escalation with a provable human-agreement guarantee (Large)

**What:** The paper "Trust or Escalate: LLM Judges with Provable Guarantees for Human Agreement" structures a judge as a cascade: a cheap/weak model judges first, and the query only escalates to a stronger, more expensive model when the cheap judge's own confidence is insufficient — while the overall system still provides a provable statistical guarantee (a PAC/RCPS-style, binomial-tail-bound confidence construction) that its automated decisions match human judgments at a level the operator specifies in advance, not just an empirical agreement rate reported after the fact.

**Why it matters for Kelvran specifically:** this is a different shape from both of Kelvran's current judge-panel options — the shipped flat, same-vendor 2-judge Bedrock panel (majority-reduced, fail-closed on tie) and the still-open `PRD.md` question of whether a future v2 skeptic panel should be "pairwise-comparison-based or independent-refutation-based." Cascaded escalation is a third axis entirely (cost-tiered, confidence-gated, with its own formal calibration guarantee) that neither existing option or open question currently names.

**Consistent with settled decisions?** Genuinely new, not a revisit — `PRD.md`'s open-questions list names only the pairwise-vs-independent-refutation choice for a future skeptic panel; a cascaded/escalating structure with a provable calibration guarantee is a different, unnamed design axis. Note the paper predates this research window by roughly two years (July 2024 / EMNLP 2024 Findings) — its content is accurately described here, but it is a structurally-new-to-Kelvran finding rather than a brand-new-to-the-field one; weigh the "genuinely new" framing accordingly.

**2026 best practice grounding:**
- "we propose Cascaded Selective Evaluation, where we use cheaper models as initial judges and escalate to stronger models only when necessary — again, while still providing a provable guarantee of human agreement" [arxiv.org/abs/2407.18370 — confirmed 2-1, primary arXiv abstract metadata].
- The formal guarantee is a real theorem, not marketing framing: the paper derives an exact binomial-tail-bound (Clopper–Pearson-style) confidence construction giving `P(R(λ̂) ≤ R⁺(λ̂) ≤ α) ≥ 1−δ` for an operator-chosen `(α, δ)` — i.e. a genuine, user-configurable statistical guarantee — conditional on an i.i.d.-sampled calibration set, a caveat the paper itself discloses and stress-tests [confirmed 2-1].

**Consequential but not urgent:** the paper's own guarantee construction requires a labeled human-agreement calibration set to fit the threshold `λ̂` against — Kelvran's `regression_corpus_judge_accuracy.json` (24 cases) is a plausible seed, but likely needs to grow before a calibration-quality guarantee would be meaningful. This is a design-shape question for a future skeptic-panel RFC, not a drop-in addition to the current panel.

**Concrete next step:** a dedicated RFC evaluating whether a cascaded (cheap-first, escalate-on-low-confidence) structure should replace or supplement the flat 2-judge panel for v2, explicitly re-opening `PRD.md`'s pairwise-vs-independent-refutation question with this as a named third option, and scoping what calibration-set size the formal guarantee would need against Kelvran's own corpus.

**Effort:** Large — a genuine judge-architecture change with its own RFC; not a bounded addition to the existing panel-reducer code.

### 6. Mixture-SPRT-family e-value tests are provably conservative and uniformly improvable — a concrete, checkable algorithmic upgrade path (Large, research pass)

**What:** A second 2026 statistical-methodology paper (Brannath & Fischer, *Anytime-Valid Testing with E-Values and Confirmatory Adaptive Designs*) proves that e-value-based anytime-valid sequential tests — the family mixture-SPRT belongs to, since a mixture-SPRT is itself a mixture of likelihood-ratio martingales — are generally statistically conservative, and that this conservatism can be uniformly closed via "recursive combination tests" whenever the null distribution is known, continuous, and least-favorable, and the test does not already exhaust its significance level.

**Why it matters for Kelvran specifically:** this is a distinct claim from Finding 2 above — Finding 2 says mSPRT's *schedule* (checking every trial) is already in the power-favorable regime relative to a fixed-checkpoint alternative; this finding says the *test itself*, independent of schedule, may be leaving statistical power on the table that a recursive-combination-test wrapper could recover, specifically because Kelvran's pass/fail data is genuinely Bernoulli (a case the mSPRT RFC itself already argued satisfies mSPRT's parametric assumption exactly, not approximately) — which is precisely the "known, continuous, least-favorable null" precondition this paper's improvement construction requires.

**Consistent with settled decisions?** Genuinely new — neither `ARCHITECTURE.md` nor `DECISIONS.md` discuss e-value conservatism or recursive combination tests; the mSPRT RFC's own "Alternatives Considered" section evaluates only Howard/Ramdas-style nonparametric confidence sequences as a rival family, never an improvement construction layered on top of mSPRT itself.

**2026 best practice grounding:**
- "sequential e-values can be uniformly improved by recursive combination tests, if they do not exhaust the level and have known and continuous least favorable conditional null distributions" [arxiv.org/abs/2606.00878 — confirmed 3-0, primary arXiv paper, Univ. of Bremen].
- The paper's Appendix A gives the concrete mechanism: for a known least-favorable null and a continuously-distributed e-value `E1`, the reciprocal `1/E1` is a strictly-conservative p-value, from which a recursive combination test can be built that provably improves on the original e-value test — and Remark 5 generalizes the underlying conservatism claim even to non-continuous e-values, at the cost of losing the improvement construction's exact form.

**Concrete next step (research pass, not a build):** a dedicated design pass checking whether Kelvran's specific null (a fixed `baseline_pass_rate`, per the mSPRT RFC) is genuinely "known, continuous, and least-favorable" in this paper's technical sense for the one-sample Bernoulli case, and if so, scoping what a recursive-combination-test wrapper around `mixture_sprt_early_stop` would look like and how much trial-count reduction it would actually buy on Kelvran's own corpora — before writing any code.

**Effort:** Large — this is explicitly a research/design pass, not an immediate implementation; the underlying construction is real and checkable, but applicability to Kelvran's specific (discrete Bernoulli, not continuous) null needs verifying before any code is written, matching this report's own "worth tracking, not immediately building" convention for high-value-but-unproven items.

### 7. Self-hostable, weight-merged open-weight judge models — a real way to diversify away from same-vendor correlation, at the cost of the tradeoff's own stated rationale (Medium–Large, follow-on)

**What:** Prometheus 2 is built by weight-merging two evaluator LMs fine-tuned separately for the two dominant judging modes (direct/absolute assessment and pairwise ranking) rather than training one model to do both jointly. It is self-hostable, open-weight (Apache 2.0), built on Mistral-7B/Mixtral-8x7B base models — architecturally unrelated to Claude — and explicitly positioned as a transparent, controllable, affordable alternative to proprietary judges.

**Why it matters for Kelvran specifically:** `DECISIONS.md`'s 2026-09-08 entry names the current 2-judge Bedrock Sonnet-5+Haiku-4.5 panel's same-vendor composition as "an explicit, accepted same-vendor-correlation tradeoff for AWS-only operational simplicity" — i.e., Kelvran already knows and has accepted the correlation risk in exchange for staying inside one operational surface. Prometheus 2 is the one concrete, verified option this research surfaced that would genuinely break that correlation (a disjoint model family, not just a disjoint deployment of the same vendor's models) — but doing so means self-hosting a third judge outside AWS/Bedrock entirely, which directly undoes the "AWS-only operational simplicity" half of the rationale that made the current tradeoff acceptable in the first place. This is a real tension worth naming explicitly, not a free win.

**Consistent with settled decisions?** Directly engages, without contradicting, the 2026-09-08 same-vendor-correlation decision — that decision named the tradeoff and accepted it for a stated reason; this finding supplies the first concretely-evaluated way to relax it, and the cost of doing so.

**2026 best practice grounding:**
- "We propose a recipe based on merging the weights of two evaluator LMs trained separately... one model fine-tuned on the Feedback Collection for direct assessment... and another fine-tuned on the Preference Collection for pairwise ranking," reported to outperform jointly training one model on both formats in the paper's own ablation [arxiv.org/abs/2405.01535 — confirmed 3-0].
- Base models and license are stated directly in the paper's own methodology/appendix: `mistralai/Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2` and `mistralai/Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct-v0.1`, released "under the Apache 2.0 license," explicitly motivated by "concerns including transparency, controllability, and affordability" relative to proprietary judges [confirmed 2-1].

**Concrete next step:** not an immediate build — flag as a candidate third panelist (or standalone alternative judge) for a future evaluation specifically measuring whether adding a genuinely disjoint-vendor judge changes panel agreement/tie rates on `regression_corpus_judge_accuracy.json` versus the current same-vendor pair, and separately scope the self-hosting operational cost (GPU inference infra outside Bedrock) against that benefit before deciding.

**Effort:** Medium–Large — a real follow-on requiring new self-hosted inference infrastructure, not a drop-in provider addition like the Bedrock/Anthropic/OpenAI functions in `judge/providers.py` today.

## Caveats

- Six of the ten confirmed claims rest on a 2-1 (not unanimous 3-0) vote: both halves of the "Trust or Escalate" cascaded-judge finding (Finding 5), the AlpacaEval Length-Controlled finding (Finding 3), Prometheus 2's OSS-alternative-positioning claim (part of Finding 7 — its weight-merging construction claim itself was 3-0), and Braintrust's TypeSafe/Jev integration (part of Finding 4 — DeepEval's own integration was 3-0). All six are still rated high-confidence by the verifying agent on direct primary-source grounds; flagged here for transparency, matching this report series' own convention.
- 15 claims were explicitly refuted during adversarial verification, clustering into four groups: (a) rival or improved anytime-valid constructions to mSPRT that did not hold up — a "testing by betting" framework's claimed superiority over Chernoff-style and supermartingale-generalized bounds (arxiv.org/abs/2010.09686, both sub-claims refuted), a "safe testing" framework's optional-continuation and GRO-optimality claims (arxiv.org/abs/1906.07801, both refuted), an "Anchored E-Watermarking" construction (arxiv.org/abs/2602.17608, refuted), a finite-horizon-optimal e-value design claimed to beat GRO-optimal e-values (arxiv.org/abs/2605.28653, refuted), and a narrower sub-claim from the very paper behind Finding 2 above that group-sequential has the *highest* power under its own baseline (non-continuous) schedule (refuted 1-2 — the schedule-*reversal* claim in Finding 2 survived; this blanket-ranking framing did not); (b) multi-judge-panel-diversity and bias-taxonomy claims — PoLL's diverse-panel-beats-single-judge claim and its intra-model-bias framing (arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796, both refuted), JudgeLM's position/knowledge/format bias taxonomy (arxiv.org/abs/2310.17631, refuted), and an AlpacaEval cross-vendor judge-consistency claim (refuted, notably from the same repo Finding 3 draws its *confirmed* LC win-rate claim from); (c) Inspect AI hardening claims targeting the sandbox/panel layers directly — sandbox checkpoint/restore hardening (v0.3.264) and strict-majority panel-tie-handling (v0.3.262), both refuted, meaning **no genuinely new finding survived on the Docker-sandboxed rollout execution model itself**; (d) DeepEval's G-Eval logprob-weighted scoring technique (refuted, 0-3).
- Time-sensitivity: the Sokolova & Sokolov and Brannath & Fischer statistical papers (Findings 2 and 6) and the DeepEval/Braintrust TypeSafe integration (Finding 4) are all from 2026 and squarely current. "Trust or Escalate" (Finding 5) and Prometheus 2 (Finding 7) are both from mid-2024 — roughly two years old — and are reported here as *structurally new to Kelvran's own documented design space*, not as brand-new-to-the-field 2026 developments; weigh that distinction when prioritizing.
- Finding 3's note about `THREAT_MODEL.md`'s Judge Tampering row naming "position/verbosity/self-preference" as what `--judge-debias` catches is this report's own mechanism-level reading (a swap-and-compare-for-disagreement check cannot structurally catch an order-independent preference), not a separately sourced/voted adversarial claim — flagged as analysis, not verified evidence, and worth a maintainer's own confirmation before treating it as settled.
- Several verification passes disclosed being unable to run a broader adversarial web search (rate-limited tooling) and relied instead on direct primary-source text/code matching — the strongest available check, but narrower than a full disconfirmation sweep in a few cases (noted per-claim above where it applied).
