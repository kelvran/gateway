# Kelvran Evals — Pre-Traffic Regression-Dataset Bootstrapping Research

**Date:** 2026-09-07
**Scope:** How to cold-start Kelvran's first real regression-tier `EvalCase` set given zero production/customer traffic. Covers sourcing strategy only — not statistical sizing, taxonomy/maintenance workflow, or judge-panel methodology, each the subject of a separate parallel research pass.
**Method:** Adversarial multi-source research (3-vote verification per claim) against real, documented practice on synthetic eval-case generation, hand-authored/threat-model-driven regression suites, public-benchmark import, and dogfooding-as-bootstrap: two 2025-2026 peer-reviewed papers on synthetic-eval quality (ACL Findings EMNLP 2025; ICLR 2026), one 2026 preprint on the LLM-as-judge test-oracle problem, one NeurIPS 2025 paper on search-time benchmark contamination, Hamel Husain's practitioner eval-methodology writing (2024-2026), Braintrust's and EvalView's documented operating models, and MongoDB's own dogfooding postmortem. This is one of four parallel deep-research passes on building Kelvran's regression-tier eval dataset (methodology/sourcing, statistical sizing, pre-traffic bootstrapping strategy, taxonomy/maintenance workflow); it does not touch or reference implementation work on the other three.

---

## Context this research does not re-litigate

Kelvran's own `EvalCase` (`evals/evals/models.py`) is deliberately narrow today:

```
EvalCase { id, revision, task_spec: dict, reference: str | None,
           tier: golden | regression | drift_sample, tags: list[str], flaky: bool }
Score    { eval_case_id, eval_case_revision, run_id: str | None, scorer_id,
           scorer_type: deterministic | llm_judge | llm_judge_panel,
           value: bool, rationale, rubric_axis, cost_usd, tier, tags, flaky }
```

The two real, checked-in fixtures (`evals/tests/fixtures/golden_example.json`, `ci_gate_example.json`) show what a case looks like *today, at real scale zero*: `task_spec` is either `{output, match: "exact", ...}` (a pre-baked string, never executed — `evals run` checks it straight against `reference`) or `{output, match: "regex", pattern}`. Every real case in the repo right now is a trivial deterministic string/regex match with an optional `reference`; the CI-gate fixture adds a `tags: ["category:safety"]` case and a `flaky: true` case, but still as pre-baked strings, not executed rollouts. This matters directly for this research's findings below: Kelvran has two structurally different kinds of "case" to bootstrap, not one —

1. **Deterministic string/regex cases** (`task_spec.output` baked in, no execution) — cheap to hand-author, cheap to synthesize, but a synthetically-generated `reference` here *is* the pitfall Finding 1-3 describe, because there is no independent oracle checking whether the generated "expected" string is actually right.
2. **Executed agentic cases** (`evals rollout` → `run_in_sandbox()` → a real `Run`, scored by `judge()`'s single LLM judge, optionally per-axis via `--judge-axes`, e.g. `rubric_axis: "correctness"`/`"safety"`) — this is the shape Kelvran's own architecture is actually built around (Rollout Scheduler, sandbox tiering, harness-transparency), but has zero real non-toy examples checked in today.

Also real and load-bearing for the recommendation below: `evals promote --run-id <id> --tier {regression,drift_sample}` already exists and joins a real `Run`+`Score`+source `EvalCase` into a new frozen regression-tier `EvalCase` — this is Kelvran's own, already-built "dogfooding → regression case" mechanism, but it only works for failures that went through the Rollout Scheduler (i.e., something with a `Run` behind it). `evals ingest --source s3://...|gs://...` maps sampled `gatewayevents_v1` production events into synthetic `drift_sample`-tier cases with `Run.stdout=""`/`EvalCase.reference=None` (gatewayevents carries no prompt/completion content) — but per `STATUS.md`, there is no live traffic flowing through this path yet; it is mechanically real and functionally idle. CI (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`'s `evals` job) already runs `evals report --fail-under` against `ci_gate_example.json` as "a deliberately-scoped dogfooding smoke test against the existing tiny fixture, not a curated regression dataset" (`STATUS.md`, Phase 1e) — i.e., Kelvran is *already* doing the smallest possible version of dogfooding-as-bootstrap, just not yet at the scale or on the case-shape (executed rollouts) the architecture is designed for.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. Synthetic generation is a real, credible cold-start tactic specifically *because* there's no traffic yet — but it is a documented capability gap, not a drop-in substitute, and the gap is invisible by inspection

**What:** Practitioner guidance directly and explicitly recommends synthetic data for exactly Kelvran's situation: "Use synthetic data to start error analysis before you have enough production traffic" (Hamel Husain & Shreya Shankar's evals FAQ, updated 2026-09-01 — six days before this research). A second practitioner source frames this even more strongly for zero-user products: "Bootstrapping Your AI With Synthetic Data Is Effective (Even With Zero Users)," citing Bryan Bischof (former Head of AI at Hex): "LLMs are surprisingly good at generating excellent — and diverse — examples of user prompts." Both are practitioner testimony, not controlled studies.

The controlled-study evidence is a real counterweight, not a contradiction of *viability* but a hard constraint on *fidelity*: a peer-reviewed ACL Findings/EMNLP 2025 paper generating LLM variants of two established reasoning benchmarks (CondaQA for negation, DROP for quantity reasoning) found the synthetic versions could be made valid per the original annotation guidelines, but were **systematically less challenging** for LLMs than the human-authored originals, **did not preserve the same relative model rankings**, and — the sharpest finding for regression-testing purposes specifically — **these differences were not visible just from inspecting the generated data**. A follow-up point in the same paper rules out the most obvious explanation: the ease gap is *not* simply the generating model being biased toward content it itself can solve ("bias does not explain why synthetic benchmarks are easier"), and synthetic-vs-human accuracy differences did not reliably predict which of two real models would perform better on the actual human benchmark.

**Why it matters for Kelvran specifically:** this cuts two ways depending on which of Kelvran's two case shapes is being bootstrapped. For deterministic string/regex cases, "systematically easier and non-representative" is a second-order concern (the pass/fail bar is exact-match, not graded difficulty). For the executed-agentic, LLM-judged case shape Kelvran's architecture actually targets, this is a first-order risk: a synthetically-bootstrapped regression suite could report a healthy pass rate while measuring a systematically easier task distribution than what the gateway/agent harness will actually face — and, per the paper's own finding, this would not be visible by having a human skim the generated `task_spec` entries before merging them.

**Confidence:** High — two independent primary sources for the cold-start recommendation (3-0 each), one peer-reviewed paper for the fidelity-gap finding (3-0, corroborated by a second, independently-voted claim on the same paper).

### 2. Two distinct, real synthetic-generation pitfalls compound when the same or a related LLM both generates cases and judges them — one of which has already fabricated a false empirical result at N=500 scale

**What:** "Preference Leakage" (arXiv:2502.01534, accepted ICLR 2026) empirically confirms that LLM-as-judge evaluators are biased toward outputs from "student" models related to the data-generating LLM, across three precise relatedness mechanisms: being the same model, an inheritance relationship (fine-tuned descendant), or membership in the same model family. This directly names the exact combination Kelvran's own architecture already guards against by design (`llm_judge`'s stated bias mitigation: "judge-model ≠ policy-model") — but that mitigation is about judge-vs-*policy*-model, not judge-vs-*generator*-model; if Kelvran ever synthesizes cases with the same LLM family it later uses as its judge, this is a live, undefended path for the same contamination class.

Separately and more concretely: a 2026 preprint on "The Test Oracle Problem in Synthetic LLM-as-Judge Corpora" (arXiv:2607.13707) demonstrates that LLM-generated negative examples (e.g., a hallucinated wrong answer paired with a correct one, generated to test judge behavior) carry **no mechanical way to verify the item is actually a valid "wrong answer" rather than degenerate garbage** — unlike deterministic perturbation of a gold answer, which carries a built-in oracle via a trivial gold-vs-negative string comparison. This is not theoretical: a shared `max_tokens=5` decoding-budget bug silently truncated one producer's synthetic hallucinated answers, fabricating an entirely fake finding — a 32-point cross-lingual accuracy collapse in a judge model — that **replicated consistently from N=50 to N=500 and survived a full mechanistic explanation and a controlled producer-swap experiment**, none of which was real. Only manual reading of the raw generations caught it; no statistical check did. A parallel positive control on a perturbation-based corpus (which does carry an oracle) caught an equivalent injected fault at 100% via a single `if gold == negative` comparison.

**Why it matters for Kelvran specifically:** this is the concrete, mechanistic version of Finding 1's "invisible by inspection" warning, applied to Kelvran's own case shape. Kelvran's deterministic string/regex cases *already have* the oracle-bearing property the test-oracle paper says perturbation-based corpora have (an exact/regex `reference` comparison is itself a cheap, built-in oracle) — this is a real, structural advantage of Kelvran's simplest case type that should not be discarded by generating both `task_spec.output` *and* `reference` synthetically without a human-verified anchor. Kelvran's LLM-judged, no-`reference` holistic cases have no such oracle at all, and neither does a synthetically-generated hallucination/adversarial case scored by `llm_judge` — that is exactly the oracle-free configuration the test-oracle paper's fabricated-32-point-collapse case study describes. Replication at scale (N=500) is explicitly *not* evidence a synthetic-generation pipeline is sound.

**Confidence:** High for both papers' own claims (each independently 3-0 or 2-1 with strong primary-source corroboration); the mapping onto Kelvran's specific case shapes above is this report's own reasoning, not a claim either paper makes about Kelvran.

### 3. The documented mitigation is procedural, not just "be careful": generate inputs, not outputs, and sequence synthetic generation strictly after — never before — real-failure-mode discovery

**What:** The most direct, repeated mitigation across practitioner sources is a concrete rule, not a vague caution: "Generate user inputs, not outputs... Use LLMs to generate realistic user queries or inputs, not the expected AI responses... This prevents your synthetic data from inheriting the biases or limitations of the generating model" (Hamel Husain's field guide). A separate piece by the same author states the sequencing explicitly: "Once you understand your real failure modes, you can use synthetic data to expand your test coverage" — synthetic data is framed as a coverage-*expansion* supplement gated on real failure modes already being identified, not a substitute for real usage data when none exists yet.

The concrete recommended procedure (same source) is hybrid, not pure generation: hand-write ~20 tuples first (to build domain intuition), scale via a two-step LLM process (generate structured tuples, then convert tuples-to-queries in a *separate* prompt to avoid repetitive phrasing), run the resulting synthetic queries through the actual system to capture full execution traces, and treat a pool of roughly 100 diverse traces as a useful starting point for failure discovery — annotating at least the first 30 by hand.

**Why it matters for Kelvran specifically:** this reconciles Finding 1's "recommended for zero traffic" with Finding 2's "generator/judge contamination and no-oracle risk" — the resolution is not "don't use synthetic data pre-traffic" but "generate the `task_spec` scenario, never the `reference`/expected verdict, and always run it through the real harness before trusting it." For Kelvran's executed-agentic case shape this maps cleanly: an LLM can generate diverse agent-task *descriptions* to feed into `evals rollout`, but a human (not the generator) should set `reference` or judge the resulting trace before a case is promoted past `drift_sample`/scratch status. For Kelvran's deterministic string/regex cases, this rule argues against synthetically generating `{output, reference}` pairs wholesale, since there the "output" *is* effectively the expected answer with no execution step to catch a garbage pairing.

**Confidence:** High — all three claims are direct, verbatim-verified statements from the same practitioner's current (updated within the research window), widely-cited methodology writing (3-0, 3-0, 2-1).

### 4. Hand-authored, threat-model-driven regression cases are built from sourcing channels chosen specifically for non-overlapping blind spots — and the one channel most teams lean on hardest is structurally unavailable pre-traffic

**What:** Braintrust's documented red-teaming methodology explicitly combines four sourcing channels for adversarial/regression cases — in-house red teamers (target product-specific risks), security vendors/consultancies (concentrated testing across agreed attack categories), open-source scanning tools (Garak, PyRIT, Promptfoo, each automating a different attack-strategy family), and production incidents (support tickets, flagged conversations, abuse reports, human review escalations) — "because combining all four sources reduces reliance on the assumptions of a single testing method." The production-incidents channel is explicitly credited with surfacing "attacks under conditions that pre-deployment testing did not reproduce."

That fourth channel is, by definition, unavailable to a product with zero production/end-user traffic: a production incident requires production traffic to exist. This is not something the source itself addresses (it does not discuss pre-launch products), but it follows directly and unavoidably from what a "production incident" is.

**Why it matters for Kelvran specifically:** Kelvran is exactly this case — real internal dogfooding and CI usage, zero external traffic. Of Braintrust's four channels, three remain fully available to Kelvran today (in-house authorship by whoever knows the gateway/cache/evals codebase best, i.e., domain-expert-driven case design grounded in `THREAT_MODEL.md`'s own named risk classes — cross-tenant isolation, cache poisoning/hijacking, prompt injection — plus open-source scanning tools like Garak/PyRIT/Promptfoo for the adversarial-input subset of cases). The fourth (production incidents) has a real, already-partially-built substitute inside Kelvran's own architecture: `evals promote --tier regression` mining a *dogfooding/CI* failure (a `Run` that failed during Kelvran's own development or CI execution) plays the same structural role production-incident mining would play once traffic exists — it is dev-time-equivalent, not a perfect substitute, but a genuine, already-mechanically-real one.

**Confidence:** High for the four-channel taxonomy and the production-incidents description (2-1, primary source directly verified); the "definitionally unavailable pre-traffic" inference and the Kelvran-specific substitute-mapping are this report's own reasoning layered on the sourced fact, not claims Braintrust itself makes.

### 5. Converting a real failure into a regression asset is a named, motivated operating pattern — and the cost of skipping this step is that the same bug resurfaces silently later

**What:** EvalView's own operating model formalizes a "bug-to-eval rule": "Every meaningful failure should become one of: a new regression test / a stronger existing test / a new behavior tag / a documented invariant... The system compounds when bugs become assets." Separately, Braintrust's own regression-testing guidance identifies the specific failure mode of *not* doing this: "The recurrence problem starts when incident review and eval coverage stay disconnected" — the same failure mode can reappear later "through a different user input, a prompt change, a model swap, a retrieval update, or an agent workflow change," because the original fix was patched in code but never captured as a reusable, re-runnable test.

**Why it matters for Kelvran specifically:** Kelvran's own real development history already contains exactly the raw material this pattern consumes — real bugs found during real internal use, each with its own RFC and fix, but (per direct inspection of `evals/ARCHITECTURE.md` and `evals/evals/models.py`) none currently exist as `EvalCase` entries. Concrete, real examples already in this repo's own history that fit the "bug-to-eval" pattern precisely: the `run_in_sandbox()` timeout bug where killing the local `docker run` CLI process did not stop the container itself (found by testing against a real Docker daemon, fixed via `--cidfile`); the cache-hit cost-double-counting bug (`finalize` re-recording cost on every repeat cache hit, not just the first real call); the OTel Metrics `PeriodicReader.Shutdown` behavior gap (always flushes, unlike the traces exporter) found while wiring a live counter. None of these currently have a corresponding `EvalCase`/`Score` guarding against recurrence — each was fixed and covered by a Go/pytest unit test in its own subsystem, but the "bug-to-eval" pattern's specific claim is that an *eval-suite* asset (not a unit test) is what keeps a fix visible across the harness-transparency comparisons `evals report` is built to run.

**Confidence:** High — the EvalView rule is a direct, verbatim-verified quote from a real, active repository's own documentation (2-1); the Braintrust recurrence-mechanism claim is verbatim-verified and, per its own verifier, even more precisely supported by the source than the original claim asked for (3-0). Both are prescriptive/aspirational documentation of an operating principle, not empirical proof of outcomes at scale — a caveat that matters for how strongly this finding should be weighted, not a reason to discount the mechanism itself, which is standard software-regression-testing logic applied to LLM/agent systems.

### 6. Dogfooding on real internal infrastructure catches concrete bug classes that synthetic/benchmark-only testing plausibly would not — with two named, real, recent examples

**What:** MongoDB's own documented practice of running release candidates on its internal production infrastructure (its own Atlas-hosted internal services, including the Evergreen CI system handling 1B CRUD ops/day) before customer release surfaced two real bugs directly attributable to this practice: a crash-on-primary-election bug (SERVER-94487) triggered by a pre-existing, organically-occurring malformed index specification that only became invalid once validation was strengthened — the kind of bug that requires *real, accumulated, imperfect production data* to exist at all, not a clean synthetic or benchmark corpus; and a 5x query-scan regression from a new index-pruning feature (SERVER-94741) that was only visible via production monitoring graphs showing a discontinuous jump under real query-shape distributions, not via any offline test suite.

**Why it matters for Kelvran specifically:** this is real, documented precedent — from a comparable-in-kind (though larger-scale) infra product — for exactly the theory behind Kelvran's own "dogfooding CI smoke test" (Phase 1e) and the `evals promote`/`evals ingest` mechanisms: bugs surfaced by real usage of one's own system, on one's own real (if internal) infrastructure, catch failure classes that a hand-designed or synthetically-generated test corpus is structurally unlikely to reproduce, because they depend on organically-accumulated state (a malformed index nobody deliberately wrote) or on real-world input-distribution shape (a query-shape mix nobody would think to synthesize). Note the honest limit on this evidence: a separate claim that MongoDB explicitly converts dogfooding bugs into expanded *automated test coverage* did not survive adversarial verification (refuted 0-3) — the documented postmortem shows dogfooding *finding* real bugs, not a documented, systematic bug-to-regression-test pipeline at MongoDB specifically. That systematic-conversion argument is instead independently supported by Finding 5's EvalView/Braintrust sources, not by MongoDB's own postmortem.

**Confidence:** High for the two named bugs and their real-infrastructure discovery mechanism (3-0, verified against the primary MongoDB engineering postmortem with linked JIRA tickets); explicitly not evidence that MongoDB itself systematizes bug-to-regression-test conversion — do not over-cite this source for that adjacent claim.

### 7. Public-benchmark import carries a real, quantified contamination risk for any agent with web-search access — but the narrower "wrong capability" risk could not be independently substantiated for Kelvran's domain in this pass

**What:** Search-Time Contamination (arXiv:2508.13180, NeurIPS 2025) is a real, quantified, replicated phenomenon: web-search-enabled agents directly retrieve benchmark ground-truth answers from public repositories (e.g., HuggingFace dataset pages) during task execution on benchmarks like Humanity's Last Exam and SimpleQA — approximately 3% of queries leaked directly, and blocking access to the leaking sources dropped agent accuracy on the affected subset by roughly 15%, established via counterfactual validation (blocking the same sources on the same previously-contaminated subset) rather than mere correlation. A 2026 follow-up independently corroborated the pattern across additional benchmarks.

This research pass could **not** independently substantiate the narrower, arguably more relevant-to-Kelvran risk — that public benchmarks test a *different capability* than what a specific product actually needs to regression-test — at the rigor this report requires: a specific claim about OpenAI's manual SWE-bench Verified audit finding frontier models reproducing the original human-written bug fix verbatim (direct contamination evidence) did not survive adversarial verification (refuted, 1-2), nor did an adjacent claim about 59.4% of audited SWE-bench Verified problems having flawed test cases. Both would have been directly on-point for this sub-question had they held up; they did not, and are reported here as refuted rather than cited as if confirmed.

**Why it matters for Kelvran specifically:** Search-Time Contamination is a live, structural risk *if* Kelvran's executed-agentic cases are ever run through a rollout harness with web-search tool access and scored against a public benchmark's known answer — a real, non-hypothetical failure mode to design against (e.g., blocking benchmark-source domains in the sandbox network policy) if public benchmarks are ever imported. But the broader, more load-bearing argument against benchmark import for Kelvran — that generic agent/LLM benchmarks (SWE-bench-style coding tasks, HLE-style knowledge tasks) test a different capability than Kelvran's own actual regression-worthy domain logic (cache-hit correctness, budget/cost enforcement, fallback-chain health-probing, cross-tenant isolation) — is this report's own domain-fit reasoning based on direct inspection of Kelvran's real bug history (Finding 5's examples are all gateway/cache business-logic bugs, not agent-capability failures), not a claim independently verified against external sources in this research pass.

**Confidence:** High for Search-Time Contamination itself (3-0, peer-reviewed, replicated); the domain-fit argument against benchmark import for Kelvran specifically is unverified inference from this pass, flagged as such rather than presented as sourced.

## Recommendation for Kelvran's first real batch

Given Kelvran's actual position — real internal dogfooding and CI usage (Phase 1e's smoke test, a real development history full of real, RFC'd bug fixes), a partially-built `evals promote`/`evals ingest` pipeline that is mechanically real but has no live production traffic to feed it yet, and an `EvalCase` shape that bifurcates into cheap deterministic string/regex cases vs. an intended-but-unpopulated executed-agentic case shape — the evidence above supports a **specific, sequenced combination**, not a single strategy:

1. **Mine Kelvran's own development history first (dev-time dogfooding, the pre-traffic substitute for Braintrust's "production incidents" channel).** Walk `docs/agents/LOGS.md`, `DECISIONS.md`, and the RFC history for every real bug found during this project's own development (the container-timeout bug, the cache cost-double-counting bug, the metrics-shutdown-flush bug, the retry-storm-mitigation composition gap, and others already named in `STATUS.md`) and convert each into a hand-authored `EvalCase` reproducing the exact failure scenario, following the bug-to-eval pattern (Finding 5) and matching the discipline MongoDB's dogfooding practice demonstrates catches real, non-synthetic bug classes (Finding 6). For the subset that already ran through the Rollout Scheduler with a real `Run`+`Score` behind them, use the already-built `evals promote --tier regression` mechanism directly — no new engineering needed. For the larger subset that are gateway-side (Go) business-logic bugs with no `Run` behind them, hand-author a new `EvalCase` whose `task_spec` reproduces the scenario a gateway-integration test already covers, scored deterministically where the failure is a clear right/wrong (e.g., "does the fallback chain skip an unhealthy deployment") or via `llm_judge`'s `--judge-axes` where it's a graded judgment call — this is genuinely domain-expert/threat-model-driven authoring (Finding 4's in-house channel), grounded in `THREAT_MODEL.md`'s own named risk classes, not generic advice.
2. **Supplement with open-source adversarial scanning tools for the safety/injection-adjacent subset.** `THREAT_MODEL.md` already names cross-tenant isolation, cache poisoning/hijacking, and prompt injection as Kelvran's highest-priority risk classes; Garak/PyRIT/Promptfoo-style automated probes (Finding 4) are a cheap, available-today source for this category specifically, independent of production traffic.
3. **Use synthetic generation only for the executed-agentic case shape's coverage gaps, and only for inputs, never references or verdicts.** Once (1) and (2) establish a real, human-verified seed set, use an LLM to generate diverse agent-*task descriptions* (the `task_spec` for `evals rollout`) to expand breadth — following the hand-seed-then-scale procedure (Finding 3): ~20 hand-written tuples first, LLM-scaled tuple generation, run every synthetic task through the real `run_in_sandbox()` harness to capture an actual trace, and target roughly 100 diverse traces as a first working pool, annotating the first 30 by hand before trusting any of them as a `regression`-tier case. Never let the same generation pass also produce the `reference` or the pass/fail verdict (Finding 3's core mitigation) — and never let the LLM used to generate a case be from the same model family as the judge that will score it (Finding 2's preference-leakage mechanism), which for Kelvran's already-dual-provider (Anthropic + OpenAI) judge wiring is a cheap, already-available constraint to enforce, not a new capability to build.
4. **Do not import public agent/LLM benchmarks as a bootstrapping source at this stage.** The domain mismatch is the load-bearing reason (Finding 7's own-reasoning caveat, not independently re-verified externally in this pass): Kelvran's actual regression-worthy surface, per its own real bug history, is gateway/cache business logic (budget, cache correctness, routing/failover, cross-tenant isolation) that no generic coding- or knowledge-benchmark measures. If a rollout harness with web-search tool access is ever built, Search-Time Contamination (Finding 7) is a concrete, real risk to design against before, not after, adopting any public benchmark.
5. **Leave the `evals ingest`/`drift_sample` production path exactly as built, and revisit sourcing strategy once it produces real data.** No change is recommended here — this is mechanically real and correctly idle; per this research's Finding 4, the production-incidents channel simply does not exist yet for Kelvran, and the honest thing to do is keep the mechanism ready (as already built) rather than force synthetic data into the `drift_sample` tier to simulate traffic that doesn't exist.

This sequencing directly answers the "how fast" half of the original question: steps 1-2 require zero new infrastructure (the promotion mechanism and threat model already exist) and can produce a first real batch immediately; step 3 is the only step requiring new tooling (a tuple-generation script) and is explicitly scoped as a coverage-gap supplement, not the primary source, consistent with every practitioner source surveyed (Finding 3) and the closest comparable-scale precedent available (Finding 6).

## Caveats

- Two findings (production-incidents/four-channel taxonomy, EvalView's bug-to-eval rule) rest on 2-1 votes rather than unanimous 3-0 — treated as high-confidence here because the dissent in each case was a single vote against otherwise well-corroborated primary-source evidence, but flagged for transparency, matching this project's own prior research-report convention.
- Three claims directly relevant to the "public benchmark import" sub-question (SWE-bench contamination audit, SWE-bench flawed-test-case rate, and a claim that benchmark contamination risk is largely inapplicable to closed/non-web-connected systems) were **refuted** during adversarial verification (0-3, 1-2, 1-2) and are explicitly excluded from Finding 7 rather than cited as if confirmed — this leaves that sub-question's evidence thinner than the other three research angles in this report, a real gap rather than a soft-pedaled answer.
- A claim that Braintrust argues for mining production failure traces as the seed source for regression datasets (as opposed to a human-reviewed, human-curated promotion step) was also refuted (0-3) — Braintrust's actual documented position (Finding 4/5) is that trace *discovery* is human-driven and promotion always requires a human-authored `expected` value, not that raw trace mining should be the seed mechanism.
- The EMNLP 2025 synthetic-eval-quality paper (Finding 1) and the preference-leakage/test-oracle papers (Finding 2) study reasoning-benchmark and general LLM-as-judge settings, not Kelvran's specific executed-agentic-rollout-plus-harness-transparency setup — the mapping onto Kelvran's two case shapes in this report is this report's own reasoning applied to the papers' findings, not something either paper tests directly.
- The MongoDB dogfooding postmortem (Finding 6) is a single company's documented practice at a much larger scale (1B CRUD ops/day on internal infrastructure) than Kelvran's current pre-1.0, zero-customer scale — treated as directionally relevant precedent for the *mechanism* (real usage surfaces bugs synthetic tests miss), not as a claim that Kelvran will see comparable bug-discovery volume at its current scale.
- This report does not address how many cases, what statistical power, or what taxonomy/tagging discipline the resulting regression suite needs — those are explicitly the subject of the three sibling parallel research passes (methodology/sourcing, statistical sizing, taxonomy/maintenance workflow) and are out of scope here by design.

## Open questions

- Once step 1 above (mining dev history) is executed, will enough real bugs convert cleanly into `EvalCase` entries to matter, or will most of Kelvran's real gateway-side bugs turn out to require gateway-execution capabilities (not just sandboxed rollout execution) that the current Rollout Scheduler / `evals rollout` harness doesn't yet have a path to reproduce faithfully?
- Should the constraint "the LLM generating a synthetic case must not share a model family with the judge that scores it" (Finding 2/3's mitigation, mapped onto Kelvran's dual-provider judge wiring) be enforced mechanically in `evals/evals/judge/`, or left as an authoring-time discipline documented in an RFC?
- At what point (case count, or category coverage gap specifically) does synthetic generation's coverage-expansion role (step 3) become worth building the tuple-generation tooling for, versus continuing to rely on steps 1-2 alone while real usage remains internal-only?
- Should `drift_sample`-tier cases ingested once real production traffic exists (via the already-built `evals ingest` path) be treated as a fifth sourcing channel on top of this report's four, or folded into the same "production incidents" channel Braintrust's taxonomy already names — i.e., does Kelvran's specific mechanical implementation warrant its own category in the taxonomy the sibling research pass is defining?
