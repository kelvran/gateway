# Cache ROI Observability & Cost-Attribution Standards — Research (2026-09-11)

**Scope:** How mature production LLM-gateway/observability products (LiteLLM's own dashboards, Portkey, Langfuse, Helicone) present cache ROI to stakeholders in 2026, and whether a standard "cache hit-rate SLO" methodology exists that Kelvran should adopt for v2's operator-facing story. Four research questions; findings below are the claims that survived 3-vote adversarial verification, merged and organized by question. "build_now" / "not_yet" verdicts are given only where the surviving evidence actually supports one.

**Already shipped (not re-litigated):** `kelvran.cache.savings_usd` (Float64Counter, dimensioned by cache layer L1/L2/L3, computed as what the request would have cost had it not been served from cache); per-layer cache-hit/age/similarity span attributes; cross-instance-telemetry primitives; cache-token cost-accounting integrated across all 5 provider adapters (Anthropic/Bedrock cache-read/creation discounts, OpenAI/Gemini/openaicompat automatic-caching token counts); the L3 semantic-cache entity/freshness hard-gate (per `AGENTS.md`'s standing "Never weaken the entity/freshness hard-gate" rule).

---

## Executive Summary

None of the four mature products studied — LiteLLM, Langfuse, Helicone, Portkey — ship a first-class "cache hit-rate %" dashboard metric or a "$ saved" pre-computed gauge as their primary cache-ROI presentation; the actual pattern is raw counters (hits/misses/cached-tokens) queried into ratios at read time, paired at most with a cost-savings/latency-improvement side-panel (Portkey) or a separately-priced cache-token usage bucket folded into normal cost accounting (Langfuse) — hit-rate-as-vanity-metric is the industry default because nobody ships anything richer, not because operators reject it. On correctness, two 2026 peer-reviewed security papers (NDSS 2026, ICML 2026) prove hit-rate telemetry cannot detect cache-correctness failures — poisoned semantic-cache entries pass through the ordinary "hit" path at 81-89% success rates, and the tension is structural: the same locality property that maximizes hit-rate is what enables key-collision attacks — yet no vendor studied has built any correctness-tracking discipline distinct from generic response-accuracy error logging, meaning Kelvran's already-shipped entity/freshness hard-gate is ahead of, not behind, the market. LiteLLM's own three maintained reference Grafana dashboards contain zero cache panels despite LiteLLM itself supporting response caching — there is no dashboard to copy wholesale, but LiteLLM's docs do demonstrate a reusable PromQL ratio-via-division idiom Kelvran can directly repurpose for its own per-layer hit-rate and savings-vs-spend panels. On customer-facing billing, no evidence surfaced of demand for a "cache savings" line item specifically; the closest signal (FinOps Foundation) shows the industry hasn't even standardized basic AI-spend attribution to business unit/cost center yet, so a cache-specific billing line item is premature by a wider margin still.

## Findings

### Finding 1 — The real cache-ROI metric shape: raw counters + query-time ratios, not a shipped hit-rate gauge or $-saved KPI
**Confidence: high** (7 sources, primarily 3-0 primary-source votes across three independent vendors)

LiteLLM's Prometheus integration exposes exactly three raw counters for its own response cache — `litellm_cache_hits_metric`, `litellm_cache_misses_metric`, `litellm_cached_tokens_metric` — with no derived hit-rate metric and no dollar-savings metric shipped out of the box; the only cost metric on the same page (`litellm_spend_metric`) tracks total spend, not cache savings. Tellingly, LiteLLM's own docs demonstrate that a hit-rate must be computed via a PromQL division query on raw counters for the *adjacent* provider-side prompt-cache metrics (`sum(rate(litellm_input_cached_tokens_metric_total[5m])) / sum(rate(litellm_input_tokens_metric_total[5m]))`), confirming the general pattern of shipping raw counters and leaving ratios to the query layer rather than emitting them as first-class metrics.

Langfuse tracks cache-read tokens as a distinct, separately-priced usage bucket (`input_cached_tokens`, priced independently from plain input tokens) rather than as a percentage-saved or dollar-saved KPI — cached tokens are subtracted out of the input/output totals into their own field, not converted into a "savings" metric. Langfuse's built-in cost dashboard shows cost by model, cost over time, and top users/use-cases by cost, but has no cache-hit-rate panel or cache-savings line item as a first-class dashboard element anywhere in its dashboard/analytics/custom-dashboard surface. Separately — and relevant as a cautionary data point, not a dashboard-shape finding — Langfuse's OTel span processor has a live, open, unresolved bug where it ignores the OpenInference `llm.cost.total` authoritative span attribute and instead recomputes cost from partial `llm.token_count.*` fields, undercounting real-world cached-agent-SDK sessions by roughly half because cache-read/cache-creation tokens are missing from the recomputation inputs. This validates Kelvran's existing architecture choice of computing `kelvran.cache.savings_usd` directly at the adapter layer (the source of truth) rather than deriving it downstream from partial token counts — a design a peer production vendor demonstrably got wrong.

Portkey's Cache analytics tab shows "latency improvements and cost savings due to cache" (its own docs' wording) — i.e., a savings/latency side-panel, not a raw hit-rate percentage and not a correctness/SLO construct; no hit-rate, correctness, or SLO language appears anywhere on that page. Helicone enforces cache validity purely through deterministic exact-match key hashing (any parameter or content change is a miss by construction) and documents no hit-rate targets or performance guarantees anywhere on its caching page.

**Verdict: build_now.** Kelvran's `kelvran.cache.savings_usd` metric is already ahead of what any of the four vendors studied ship as a pre-computed savings signal — none of them expose a dedicated $-saved OTel/Prometheus counter; Kelvran should keep it and pair it with raw per-layer hit/miss counters (already emitted as span attributes) exported as counters too, then let hit-rate be a query-time ratio (matching universal industry practice, not a Kelvran-specific gap to fill differently). **Named trigger:** none needed — the underlying metrics already exist; the only remaining work is ensuring hit/miss are also exported as counters (not just span attributes) so the ratio is queryable in Grafana/Prometheus, which is a small, low-risk addition.

### Finding 2 — Cache-correctness SLO methodology: no industry standard exists; academic security research proves hit-rate-alone is unsafe; Kelvran's existing hard-gate is already ahead of every vendor studied
**Confidence: high** (2 independent peer-reviewed 2026 papers, both 3-0; corroborated by 4 independent vendor-absence findings)

A genuine NDSS 2026 paper (*When Cache Poisoning Meets LLM Systems*, SUSTech/ByteDance, DOI-verified) demonstrates that a semantic cache can be made to serve attacker-chosen/incorrect responses at 81-89% success rates across GPTCache and three major public clouds (AWS/Azure/Alibaba, black-box setting) while the poisoned entry still passes the cache's own similarity-threshold check and registers as a normal hit — proving hit-rate telemetry structurally cannot detect correctness failures, because the attack lives entirely inside the legitimate-hit code path. A separate, independently peer-reviewed ICML 2026 paper (arXiv 2601.23088, key-collision attack on LLM semantic caching) goes further and identifies this as a *structural*, not merely implementation-level, tension: "the locality required to maximize cache hit rates fundamentally conflicts with the cryptographic avalanche effect necessary for collision resistance" — i.e., the same property that makes a semantic cache good at its job (matching similar-but-not-identical queries) is exactly what makes it exploitable via key-collision manipulation. This directly substantiates the framing in Kelvran's own `PRD.md` that hit-rate-alone is a vanity metric without a correctness-tracking discipline.

No confirmed evidence exists that any of the four vendors studied has actually built a correctness-tracking SLO distinct from generic response-accuracy error logging: Portkey's docs mention an "Accuracy" error category in its general Errors tab, but it's not tied to cache correctness specifically and the Cache tab itself has zero hit-rate/correctness/SLO language; Helicone has no distinct cache-correctness monitoring layer beyond deterministic exact-match hashing; Langfuse's cost-tracking page treats "cache" purely as a cost-accounting term with zero mention of correctness, staleness, or SLO methodology anywhere in its docs corpus (checked across the cost-tracking page and the separate prompt-management caching page). This is a genuine industry whitespace, not a Kelvran-specific gap: nobody has shipped a reference correctness SLO to copy.

**Verdict: build_now** — but scoped narrowly, since there is no existing methodology to adopt (there is nothing to copy; this would be original work). Kelvran already has the actual defense the academic papers implicitly call for (the L3 entity/freshness hard-gate, which is explicitly protected from being weakened per `AGENTS.md`) — the missing piece is not the defense itself but *exposing its own trigger/rejection rate as a first-class metric* (e.g., a counter for entity-gate rejections or freshness-gate misses alongside the existing hit/age/similarity span attributes), which would give Kelvran a genuinely differentiated "cache-correctness signal" dashboard panel that pairs with the savings/hit-rate panels from Finding 1 — something none of the four studied vendors have. **Named trigger:** none needed beyond scheduling the metric-export work; the gate mechanism this would instrument already ships.

### Finding 3 — No existing "cache ROI" reference Grafana dashboard to copy; one reusable PromQL idiom exists and should be repurposed
**Confidence: high** (2 sources, both independently grep-verified against raw dashboard JSON, 3-0)

LiteLLM's own three maintained/shipped Grafana dashboards (`dashboard_1`, `dashboard_v2`, `dashboard_genai_otel` — the latter added July 2026 specifically to chart OTel GenAI metrics) contain zero cache-related panels, metrics, or PromQL queries anywhere — independently confirmed via `grep -ci cache` returning 0 on all three raw dashboard JSON files, and via a full recursive extraction of every panel title, which fall exclusively into Requests/Spend/Tokens/latency-percentile/rate-limit/failure buckets. This is despite LiteLLM itself actively supporting response caching (the very feature Finding 1's raw counters describe) — i.e., even the vendor whose product shape is closest to Kelvran's gateway-with-embedded-cache has never dashboarded its own cache feature in its reference implementation.

The one concrete, reusable pattern found is the ratio-via-division PromQL idiom LiteLLM's own docs demonstrate for a *different* metric pair (provider-side prompt-cache tokens, not response-cache hits): `sum(rate(litellm_input_cached_tokens_metric_total[5m])) / sum(rate(litellm_input_tokens_metric_total[5m]))`. This idiom transfers directly onto Kelvran's own telemetry shape.

**Verdict: build_now.** Ship 2-3 example Grafana panels as reference dashboards for v2, reusing the LiteLLM ratio idiom against Kelvran's own metric names — e.g. a per-layer hit-rate panel (`sum(rate(kelvran_cache_hits_total{layer="L1"}[5m])) / sum(rate(kelvran_cache_requests_total{layer="L1"}[5m]))`, repeated per L1/L2/L3), a savings-vs-spend ratio panel (`sum(rate(kelvran_cache_savings_usd_total[5m])) / sum(rate(kelvran_llm_spend_usd_total[5m]))`), and (per Finding 2) a correctness-gate-trigger-rate panel once that counter exists. This is low-effort (dashboard JSON authoring against metrics that already exist, no new instrumentation required for the first two panels) and fills a gap no studied vendor — including the one closest in shape to Kelvran — has filled. **Named trigger:** none needed; the metrics these panels query already ship.

### Finding 4 — Customer-facing "cache savings" billing line item: no evidence of demand found; premature given the industry hasn't standardized basic AI-spend attribution yet
**Confidence: medium** (1 primary source, 2-1 vote; the question itself returned no direct evidence)

No claim in this research round's confirmed or refuted sets directly addresses demand for a customer-facing "cache savings" billing line item — this sub-question did not surface a verifiable primary source either way. The closest adjacent signal: the FinOps Foundation states plainly that "there is no native concept of business unit, cost center, application, or workload" in provider AI/LLM billing exports today, and is *proposing* (not shipping) an extension of its FOCUS spec to close that gap. If the industry hasn't yet standardized even basic AI-spend attribution to a business unit or cost center, a cache-specific savings line item sits further still down the same unstandardized stack — there is no existing billing convention to hook a cache-savings figure into, and no evidence any vendor's customers are asking for one distinct from ordinary cost/spend reporting (which Langfuse, Helicone, and Portkey all already provide without a cache-specific breakout).

**Verdict: not_yet**, by absence of demand signal rather than disconfirming evidence. **Named trigger:** a specific paying customer explicitly requesting a cache-savings billing line item, or the FinOps Foundation's proposed FOCUS extension actually shipping in a form Kelvran could integrate against (currently a proposal only, per the same source).

## Additional context: OTel standardization status (relevant to how Kelvran should frame its metrics, not a standalone RQ)

OTel's GenAI semantic conventions define `gen_ai.usage.cache_read.input_tokens` and `gen_ai.usage.cache_write.input_tokens`, but both are marked **Development** stability, not **Stable** — per OTel's own versioning spec, signals in Development status may still break or be discarded entirely, so this is not yet a frozen standard Kelvran can treat as a stable target. More importantly, the standard models cache tokens purely as **token-count subsets that roll into `gen_ai.usage.input_tokens`** — there is no dollar-savings dimension anywhere in the current standardized attribute registry. An open (unmerged) OTel proposal to add per-operation cost conventions (`gen_ai.usage.cost.*`) explicitly defers per-token-class cost breakdown (including cache-read/cache-write cost) to a separate, not-yet-scoped follow-up issue — meaning even the *proposed* future OTel cost standard does not cover what `kelvran.cache.savings_usd` already does today. **Framing implication for v2 docs:** describe `kelvran.cache.savings_usd` as a Kelvran-specific extension beyond the OTel standard, not as an implementation of an existing or forthcoming OTel convention — that claim would not survive scrutiny against the primary spec.

## Caveats

- Two claims about the exact dollar delta in the Langfuse cost-undercount bug (the specific $0.52 vs $0.24 figures, and the root-cause attribution to a specific OpenInference instrumentation gap) did not survive adversarial verification and are excluded — the underlying bug (span processor ignores `llm.cost.total`, recomputes from partial token counts) is independently confirmed at 2-1 and stands on its own.
- Several broader synthesis claims about the two security papers ("hit-rate can be adversarially driven high while correctness fails," "no mature correctness-tracking discipline exists anywhere," "existing semantic caching schemes lack correctness guarantees at the matching level") were refuted even though the narrower, more precisely-scoped claims from the same papers (Findings 2's actual supporting claims) survived at 3-0 — treat the narrower framings in this report as the verified ceiling; do not extend them further without new research.
- A claim that cloud providers (AWS/Azure/Alibaba) gate semantic-cache hits *solely* on similarity threshold with *no* independent correctness layer was refuted (0-3) — do not assume those clouds have zero correctness mitigation; this research did not establish what mitigations (if any) they actually run.
- A claim that OTel issue #484 gives Kelvran "a real upstream standard to align against" was refuted (0-3) — the per-token-class cost-breakdown proposal is not a usable reference point yet, contrary to what that claim implied.
- Finding 4 is explicitly unresolved by direct evidence, not "researched and found to be a non-issue" — treat it as a backlog item for a future dedicated pass if customer demand signal ever appears, not a closed question.
- Exa/Tavily web search tools hit rate limits repeatedly during this research round; several findings rest on direct primary-source WebFetch/API verification alone rather than broader corroborating web search, which is the stronger verification method for primary claims but means less breadth-of-corroboration than a fully unconstrained search pass would have produced.

## Open Questions

1. Should the correctness-gate-trigger-rate metric proposed in Finding 2 be scoped as a simple counter (rejections/misses at the entity or freshness gate) or should it carry richer dimensions (which gate, which layer, rejection reason) from day one — this needs a short design pass against the existing gate implementation, not further research.
2. Does Kelvran's per-layer hit/miss telemetry already exist as exportable Prometheus/OTel counters, or only as span attributes today? Finding 1's and Finding 3's "build_now, no new instrumentation" verdicts assume the former; if only span attributes exist, a small counter-export addition is a prerequisite before the example Grafana panels in Finding 3 can actually be built.
3. If a customer-facing cache-savings billing line item is ever requested (Finding 4's named trigger), should it reuse the same `kelvran.cache.savings_usd` figure used internally, or does customer-facing billing require a different (more conservative/audited) computation path than an internal operator metric?
4. Is there a non-LLM-gateway industry (e.g., CDN/edge-cache observability, which has decades of hit-rate-vs-correctness practice) with a more mature correctness-SLO methodology worth a dedicated research pass, given the LLM-specific vendors studied here (LiteLLM, Langfuse, Helicone, Portkey) collectively have none?
