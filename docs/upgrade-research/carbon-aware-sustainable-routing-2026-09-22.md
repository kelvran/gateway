# Carbon-Aware & Sustainable AI Infrastructure Routing — Upgrade Research (2026-09-22)

**Date:** 2026-09-22
**Scope:** Whether carbon-aware compute scheduling/routing, model-size-aware routing for energy efficiency, and real published LLM-inference carbon-footprint data are genuine, adopted 2026 practice — and whether Kelvran gateway (`gateway/`) has a real near-term reason to build toward any of it. Kelvran's own current state, confirmed by direct grep/read: **no code anywhere in `gateway/` computes, stores, or exposes energy or carbon data** — `api/gatewayevents/v1/gatewayevents.proto` has no region or environmental-impact field, and `THREAT_MODEL.md`'s NIST AI 600-1 crosswalk already carries an explicit row: *"Environmental Impacts | Out of scope — no code addresses training/inference energy or carbon accounting; sits entirely with the upstream model providers."* This report checks whether that existing scope-out (and its stated rationale) still holds up against fresh 2026 evidence, rather than assuming it does.
**Method:** Direct primary-source verification — provider blogs/docs, regulatory text, and academic papers fetched and read directly — rather than this directory's earlier multi-agent adversarial-vote protocol. Every claim below is checked against at least one primary source; a second independent source is cross-checked and noted where relevant. ~25 primary sources fetched across cloud-provider sustainability programs, LLM gateway/router vendor documentation, EU and US regulatory text, corporate sustainability reports, and 2026 academic papers (two independent WebFetch calls against known, checkable pages — Google's own inference-footprint paper and the Green Software Foundation's SDK repo — were run first specifically to sanity-check that this sandboxed environment returns real, non-fabricated web content; both returned specific, internally consistent, independently-datable detail, not generic or byte-identical filler, so the rest of this pass proceeded on that basis). Codebase claims verified by direct grep/read against `gateway/`, `THREAT_MODEL.md`, `PRD.md`, `DECISIONS.md`, and this directory's own prior reports — confirmed no existing report in this corpus (including `ai-compliance-regulatory-readiness-2026-09-14.md`) touches carbon/environmental regulation at all; this is the first.

## Executive summary

The honest verdict is that Kelvran's existing scope-out is still basically correct, and the evidence this pass found *strengthens* rather than undermines its stated rationale. No mainstream, adopted LLM gateway (LiteLLM, Portkey, Kong AI Gateway, Envoy AI Gateway, OpenRouter — checked directly against each vendor's own docs/benchmarks) ships carbon-aware routing as a real feature; the only genuine, shipping example found anywhere in this space is a single-city, invite-only beta startup gateway (LowRouter/Carbonifer SAS) with no SLA and no visible customer base, which is real evidence a market niche exists but not evidence of settled production practice. More consequentially for Kelvran specifically: two of Kelvran's five provider adapters — OpenAI and Anthropic — publish **zero** real energy or carbon data for their models (confirmed via OpenAI's own environmental-impact page and independent 2026 financial journalism describing both companies' pre-IPO "post-ESG" silence on emissions), which means any carbon-aware feature Kelvran built today would be flying blind for the two adapters likely carrying the most traffic, forced onto third-party statistical estimation (EcoLogits-style LCA modeling) rather than provider-disclosed ground truth for at least 4 of its 5 adapters. Google is the one real exception — a genuinely rigorous, methodology-disclosed inference-footprint paper (Aug 2025) — but even that is a research disclosure about aggregate Gemini Apps usage, not a live per-response field Kelvran's `gemini` adapter could read today. On the regulatory side, the pressure this task asked about is real in direction but weaker and more indirect than a "mounting compliance requirement" framing would suggest: the EU's CSRD had its mandatory scope substantially *narrowed* in 2026 (Omnibus I), the US SEC is actively proposing full rescission of its 2024 climate-disclosure rule (never took effect), and the one AI-specific energy-disclosure law already in force — EU AI Act Article 53/Annex XI — obligates GPAI *model providers* to document training energy to regulators, not gateway operators like Kelvran, and not at inference time at all. The genuinely real, load-bearing signal is Google's own carbon-intelligent computing platform, which is the most mature carbon-aware compute-shifting system in production anywhere — and it is deliberately scoped to non-urgent, movable *batch* tasks (video processing, not request-serving inference) precisely because it cannot tolerate the latency Kelvran's synchronous chat-completion traffic requires, undercutting any assumption that a hyperscaler template exists for Kelvran to adapt directly.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. No mainstream, adopted LLM gateway offers carbon-aware routing today; the one real example is a single tiny, pre-traction beta (Small)

**What:** Direct checks against LiteLLM's own AI-gateway marketing/benchmark page, Portkey, Kong AI Gateway's own engineering blog and benchmark repo, Envoy AI Gateway's docs/talks, and OpenRouter's routing docs turned up zero carbon-related routing dimensions anywhere — every one of these routes on latency, cost, health, task-type classification, or fallback, never carbon. The only real, shipping counter-example found is **LowRouter** (`lowrouter.ai`, operated by Carbonifer SAS, Nantes, France): an OpenAI-compatible proxy across 40+ regions whose `auto` routing mode ranks candidate routes by EU-sovereignty first, then lowest grid carbon intensity (sourced from EcoLogits' inference-energy model plus Ember Climate/regional grid-operator data — RTE, PJM, CAISO, National Grid ESO, AEMO), then lowest price. Its own docs disclose it is an "invite-only beta," "early-stage, experimental service without an SLA," with several advertised features still "coming soon" — real, technically substantive, but with no visible evidence of production customer volume.

**Why it matters for Kelvran specifically:** this directly answers the research brief's core question — yes, a real LLM gateway offering carbon-aware routing exists today, but it is one pre-traction startup, not an established competitor feature Kelvran risks falling behind on.

**Consistent with settled decisions?** Consistent with, and now evidence-backed rather than assumed for, `THREAT_MODEL.md`'s "sits entirely with the upstream model providers" framing — no competitor gateway has decided otherwise either.

**2026 best practice grounding:**
- LiteLLM's own AI-gateway page, Kong's engineering blog/benchmark repo, and a third-party 2026-06 comparison of Envoy AI Gateway 1.0 all describe routing exclusively in terms of latency, cost, health/fallback, and task classification — no carbon dimension in any [litellm.ai, konghq.com, developersdigest.tech — each independently checked, no carbon mention found in any].
- LowRouter's own docs are unusually candid about the mechanism and its limits: routing priority order is configurable but carbon-before-cost is the default; missing carbon data is reported as `unknown`, never averaged or fabricated; and a prior `lowrouter/auto` mode that let the router pick the *model* (not just the route) by carbon-per-token was deliberately discontinued because it "meant the greenest answer was always the smallest model in the catalogue, a capability downgrade you had not asked for and could not see" [lowrouter.ai/docs — confirmed directly].

**Concrete next step:** none — no build action indicated. Worth a calendar reminder to re-check LowRouter's traction (or a successor) in 6-12 months, since it is currently the only real signal that this could become a competitive differentiator.

**Effort:** Small — this finding is a monitoring item, not a build item.

### 2. Kelvran's own five provider adapters split badly on real carbon data: 1 real disclosure, 4 with none or only account-level aggregates (Medium — a real blocker, not a green light)

**What:** Checked each of Kelvran's five adapters' upstream provider against what it actually discloses about inference energy/carbon:
- **openai** adapter → OpenAI: its own "Environmental Impact of AI" resource explicitly states "proprietary energy benchmarks for models like GPT-4 aren't yet published," and cites *independent* third-party estimates (Epoch AI, University of Michigan's ML Energy Initiative) rather than its own figures.
- **anthropic** adapter → Anthropic: independent 2026 financial journalism (Bloomberg, Aug 2026) reports Anthropic has disclosed no greenhouse-gas emissions data and made no net-zero pledge ahead of its IPO; its one disclosed sustainability action is joining a carbon-removal purchasing coalition (Frontier), which says nothing about inference-time carbon.
- **bedrock** adapter → AWS: AWS's Customer Carbon Footprint Tool / newer Sustainability console reports emissions at the account/region/service level from billing data — real and genuinely useful for corporate reporting, but there is no per-model, per-invocation figure a gateway could attach to a single Bedrock request.
- **gemini** adapter → Google: the one real exception — a rigorous, methodology-disclosed paper (below) — but it reports an aggregate median across "Gemini Apps" traffic in a point-in-time research disclosure, not a live per-response field the Gemini API returns today, and it carries its own real caveats (see grounding below): all twelve authors are Google employees (including Jeff Dean, Amin Vahdat, Ben Gomes), self-published with no disclosed independent audit, and part of its headline year-over-year *carbon* (not energy) improvement is explicitly attributed to market-based accounting — i.e. clean-energy procurement/renewable-energy-certificate purchasing — rather than pure operational efficiency gains.
- **openaicompat** adapter → self-hosted vLLM/Ollama: no upstream provider disclosure applies at all; any carbon estimate here would be entirely Kelvran's own responsibility (e.g., third-party LCA tooling against known hardware), same as anyone self-hosting.

**Why it matters for Kelvran specifically:** this is the single most concrete reason any carbon-aware feature is premature — it isn't a matter of Kelvran writing code to consume real signal; for 4 of 5 adapters there is no real per-request signal to consume, only third-party statistical modeling with its own disclosed uncertainty.

**Consistent with settled decisions?** Directly confirms — with evidence, not assumption — `THREAT_MODEL.md`'s stated reason for the existing scope-out ("sits entirely with the upstream model providers"): the providers mostly aren't providing it.

**2026 best practice grounding:**
- OpenAI's own resource page, dated August 2025 and last updated June 2026, is explicit about the gap in its own disclosures [academy.openai.com — confirmed, primary source].
- Bloomberg's reporting on OpenAI/Anthropic/SpaceX's "post-ESG" emissions silence ahead of IPOs is corroborated by the absence of any Anthropic/OpenAI sustainability report or GHG inventory found anywhere in this pass [bloomberg.com, 2026-08-12 — confirmed; independently consistent with OpenAI's own admission above, i.e. two independent sources agree].
- AWS's and Google Cloud's carbon-footprint tooling is documented by each vendor as account/billing-level, explicitly pitched for "regulatory reporting processes" and "investor sustainability inquiries," never as a per-request or per-model API field [docs.aws.amazon.com, aws.amazon.com/sustainability, cloud.google.com/carbon-footprint — confirmed against both vendors independently].
- Google's Gemini inference-footprint paper is a point-in-time (May 2025 data) research disclosure with an explicit caveat that "the data and claims have not been verified by an independent third-party" [arxiv.org/abs/2508.15734, via cloud.google.com/blog — confirmed, primary source]. Directly fetching the paper's own text confirms two further, real limits worth weighing before treating its headline 33x/44x figures at face value: it explicitly uses market-based (MB) emissions accounting, and states outright that "a 1.4x reduction in MB emissions intensity (gCO2e/kWh) of Google's data center electricity from workload location impacts and clean energy procurement" is one component of the claimed carbon reduction — meaning part of the improvement is renewable-energy-certificate/PPA procurement, not solely operational efficiency; and all listed authors (Elsworth, Huang, Patterson, Schneider, Sedivy, Goodman, Townsend, Ranganathan, Dean, Vahdat, Gomes, Manyika) are Google employees, with no independent audit disclosed anywhere in the paper or its accompanying Google Cloud blog post [arxiv.org/abs/2508.15734, direct fetch of the paper text — confirmed, primary source].

**Concrete next step:** none for now. If Kelvran ever revisits this space, the honest framing for any feature description would be "estimated, not measured, for the large majority of traffic" — matching LowRouter's own disclosed convention of reporting `unknown` rather than fabricating a figure.

**Effort:** Medium — sizing this as a blocker, not a build task; the "effort" is really the size of the gap that would need to close first (real provider disclosure, which is not Kelvran's to build).

### 3. The most mature real carbon-aware compute-shifting system in production anywhere is deliberately batch-only and explicitly cannot serve latency-sensitive traffic like Kelvran's (Medium)

**What:** Google's carbon-intelligent computing platform (launched 2020, extended with grid-event-triggered demand response in 2023 and explicitly extended to cover ML workloads in 2025) is real, well-documented, and directly primary-sourced across three of Google's own blog posts spanning 2020-2025. It compares a next-day Electricity Maps carbon-intensity forecast against Google's own internal power-demand forecast to shift the *timing* (and, per the 2025 post, now also the *location*) of **non-urgent, movable compute tasks** — the 2020 post names YouTube video processing, Google Photos filter generation, and Google Translate word additions as its concrete examples. The 2025 extension adds true demand-response (temporarily curtailing/rescheduling ML workload power draw during a utility-flagged grid event), now covering agreements with Indiana Michigan Power, Tennessee Valley Authority, and prior pilots with Omaha Public Power District.

**Why it matters for Kelvran specifically:** this is the single clearest piece of evidence against assuming a hyperscaler carbon-routing template is waiting for Kelvran to adopt. Google — with more scale and more internal carbon telemetry than anyone else surveyed — deliberately excludes exactly the workload class Kelvran serves (synchronous, latency-bound, user-facing request/response) from this mechanism, and says so explicitly: the system exists *because* it can be decoupled from real-time performance requirements, which a chat-completion request cannot be.

**Consistent with settled decisions?** Reinforces `THREAT_MODEL.md`'s scope-out; also directly informs `gateway/ARCHITECTURE.md`'s existing "wait for real production traffic" posture on other routing signals — carbon-aware timing/location shifting is architecturally a batch-scheduling problem, not a per-request routing problem, which is a different shape than anything else in Kelvran's router.

**2026 best practice grounding:**
- Google's 2020 announcement names Electricity Maps as its forecast partner and explicitly scopes the initial rollout to "non-urgent" tasks "without impacting the performance of Google services... that people rely on around the clock" [blog.google, 2020-04-22 — confirmed, primary source].
- The 2025 extension explicitly frames flexible demand as usable "especially" because affected workloads (video processing, ML training) can tolerate deferral — contrasted against services like Search/Maps/Cloud that cannot [blog.google, 2025-08-04 — confirmed, primary source, and consistent with the 2023 post's identical framing, i.e. two independently-dated posts from the same vendor agree].
- The Green Software Foundation's Carbon Aware SDK — the general-purpose open-source building block for this pattern — documents the same shape: its own example use case is ML *training*, citing up to 15% reduction from time-shifting and up to 50%+ from location-shifting, again a deferrable-workload framing, not real-time serving [github.com/Green-Software-Foundation/carbon-aware-sdk — confirmed, primary source].

**Concrete next step:** none. This finding is primarily useful as a design constraint to keep in mind if this space is ever revisited: any future Kelvran carbon feature would need to be evaluated against real-time latency SLOs from day one, not adapted from a batch-shifting precedent.

**Effort:** Medium — again, an analysis finding rather than a build item; recorded as Medium only because internalizing the "this doesn't transfer to real-time serving" lesson matters if the topic resurfaces.

### 4. Model-size energy variance is real and large (up to ~50-70x) and likely already tracks Kelvran's existing cost-tier signal for free — but the one real production attempt to route model *choice* on carbon was shipped and then deliberately reversed (Small-Medium)

**What:** Three independent sources converge on a large, real energy spread by model size/architecture: Hugging Face's **AI Energy Score** project (led by Sasha Luccioni et al., a public leaderboard now covering 166 models across 10 tasks) found up to a 50x spread between the most- and least-efficient models on text generation specifically; Hugging Face's own Open LLM Leaderboard emissions-analysis blog post (2,742 models analyzed) found bigger models generally cost more CO2 but with diminishing accuracy returns, and — notably — that community fine-tunes are sometimes *more* carbon-efficient than the official releases they're derived from; and an independent 2025 academic benchmark ("How Hungry is AI?") measured up to ~70x spread across 30 commercial models from OpenAI/Anthropic/Meta/DeepSeek (GPT-4.1 nano at 0.454 Wh vs. o3 at 39.223 Wh for a long prompt), while explicitly noting this is a third-party model-based *estimate*, since none of the commercial providers disclose model-specific inference data. Separately, Kelvran's own 2026-09-20 upgrade-research report already recommends a discrete cost-tier-band routing filter (OpenRouter-style) as **build_now** for cost purposes; because model cost and model compute/energy intensity correlate strongly (bigger, more expensive models are close to universally the more energy-hungry ones per the sources above), that already-recommended mechanism is very likely a reasonable *energy* proxy too, at zero additional engineering cost. Against that, LowRouter's own now-discontinued `lowrouter/auto` mode (Finding 1) is a real, disclosed cautionary precedent: it let the router pick the model itself, ranked by carbon-per-token, and was pulled specifically because it silently downgraded model choice without the caller's consent.

**Why it matters for Kelvran specifically:** this is a genuine, low-cost synergy worth naming even though nothing needs building today — if/when Kelvran ships the cost-tier-band filter already recommended in the 2026-09-20 report, it should be described (internally and to users) as *also* a reasonable energy-intensity proxy, without needing separate carbon-specific engineering.

**Consistent with settled decisions?** Additive to, not a revisit of, `docs/upgrade-research/cost-aware-cascading-tier1-2026-09-20.md`'s existing `build_now` verdict on the discrete cost-tier band; and directly reinforces the same "never let a routing mechanism silently trade away something the caller didn't ask to trade" principle already established across Kelvran's 2026-09-11/2026-09-13/2026-09-20 model-routing-intelligence reports (there, about quality; here, about model choice more broadly).

**2026 best practice grounding:**
- AI Energy Score's own announcement blog states the leaderboard "ranks 166 AI models across 10 common AI tasks" with a 1-5 star rating system modeled on household-appliance energy labels [huggingface.co/blog/sasha/announcing-ai-energy-score — confirmed, primary source].
- The Open LLM Leaderboard emissions-analysis post's own stated methodology and findings (2,742 models, diminishing accuracy-per-CO2 returns at scale, community fine-tunes sometimes beating official releases on efficiency) are both directly quoted from Hugging Face's own blog repo [github.com/huggingface/blog — confirmed, primary source].
- The "How Hungry is AI?" benchmark explicitly states commercial providers "do not disclose model-specific inference data," which is why its own 30-model comparison is a third-party estimate, not provider ground truth — directly reinforcing Finding 2 [arxiv.org/html/2505.09598v1 — confirmed, primary source].
- LowRouter's routing docs state its own reason for discontinuing model-choice-by-carbon in nearly these words: "a capability downgrade you had not asked for and could not see" [lowrouter.ai/docs/models/routing — confirmed, primary source, same source as Finding 1].

**Concrete next step:** when (not if, per the 2026-09-20 report's own verdict) Kelvran ships the cost-tier-band filter, note in its RFC/docs that it is also a reasonable energy proxy — no separate carbon-specific code path needed. Do **not** build any mechanism that lets a router silently pick a *different model* than requested on carbon grounds, mirroring both LowRouter's own reversal and Kelvran's existing quality-preservation principle.

**Effort:** Small-Medium — Small for the documentation note riding on already-planned work; Medium if ever read as license to build a dedicated carbon-model-selection feature, which this finding argues against.

### 5. Regulatory pressure specific to inference carbon is real in direction but weaker, narrower, and more indirect than "mounting compliance requirement" — and none of it reaches a gateway operator like Kelvran directly (Medium)

**What:** Three separate regulatory threads, checked against primary text:
- **EU CSRD**: the mandatory reporting scope was substantially *narrowed* in 2026, not expanded. The "Stop-the-Clock" Directive (in force since April 2025) delayed wave-2/3 companies' first reporting year by two years; the follow-on "Content Directive" (published in the Official Journal, entering force 16 March 2026, transposition due by member states by 19 March 2027) restricts mandatory CSRD scope to undertakings with **>1,000 employees and >€450M turnover** (previously "all large undertakings" plus listed SMEs), with a parallel simplification of the underlying ESRS standards (fewer datapoints, quantitative over narrative, materiality-focused). A separate voluntary standard (VSME-based) now exists for smaller "protected undertakings" specifically to cap how much sustainability data large reporters can demand from them.
- **US SEC**: the 2024 climate-disclosure rule never took effect — stayed in April 2024 pending Eighth Circuit review, the Commission voted in March 2025 to stop defending it, and on 2026-05-29 the SEC formally proposed **full rescission** of the rule in its entirety (comment period closed 2026-08-03), citing lack of statutory authority as the primary basis. As of this report's date the rule remains stayed and is headed toward repeal, not toward taking effect.
- **EU AI Act, Article 53 / Annex XI**: this is the one AI-specific energy-disclosure obligation genuinely in force today (GPAI governance rules, including Article 53, became applicable 2 August 2025). It requires GPAI *model providers* (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, etc. — not gateway/router operators) to document, in **non-public** technical documentation submitted only to the EU AI Office and national authorities, the "known or estimated energy consumption of the model" (Annex XI Section 1, point 2(e)), with point 2(d)'s compute-resource figures (e.g., training FLOPs) as the fallback basis when actual energy is unknown. This is a training-time documentation requirement aimed at regulators, not an inference-time or public disclosure, and it does not create any obligation for Kelvran, which is not a GPAI model provider.

**Why it matters for Kelvran specifically:** directly tempers the research brief's own framing question — there is no regulatory trigger here that would make carbon-aware inference routing a near-term compliance necessity for a gateway operator, in either the EU or the US, today.

**Consistent with settled decisions?** No prior Kelvran report addresses this; `ai-compliance-regulatory-readiness-2026-09-14.md` (this directory) does not mention carbon, environmental impact, CSRD, or energy at all — this is the first report in this corpus to check it, and it lands on "not a near-term driver," consistent with `THREAT_MODEL.md`'s existing scope-out.

**2026 best practice grounding:**
- The European Commission's own finance page and a law-firm (Simmons & Simmons) summary of the finalized Content Directive both independently confirm the >1,000-employee/>€450M-turnover narrowing and the 16 March 2026 entry-into-force date [finance.ec.europa.eu, assets.contentstack.io — two independent sources agree].
- The SEC's own press release, the formal Federal Register filing, and a sitting Commissioner's public statement all independently confirm the rescission proposal, its 2026-05-29 date, and the stayed/never-effective status of the underlying rule [sec.gov (press release, rule filing, and Commissioner Uyeda's statement) — three same-agency primary documents, internally consistent].
- Article 53's exact text and Annex XI's exact point 2(e) language were fetched and quoted directly from the AI Act's own article-by-article reference tool and cross-checked against the EU's own official AI Act Service Desk page, which independently confirms Article 53(1)(a)'s reference to Annex XI and the 2 August 2025 applicability date [artificialintelligenceact.eu, ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu, digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu — three independent EU-affiliated sources agree].

**Concrete next step:** none. Worth a note that this conclusion is time-sensitive in both directions — EU simplification and US rescission are both still mid-process (transposition through March 2027; SEC comment period closed but no final rule yet) — so this is a "recheck if either process concludes differently than currently proposed" item, not a permanently closed question.

**Effort:** Medium — an analysis/monitoring finding, not a build item; flagged Medium because getting this wrong (over- or under-stating regulatory urgency) has real cost if repeated in a future Kelvran compliance doc.

**Why it matters for Kelvran specifically:** the honest reading is that the demand signal, such as it is, is coming from hyperscaler-level corporate sustainability reporting (Microsoft's own operational footprint), not from any evidence that Kelvran's actual buyer (an engineering team choosing an LLM gateway) is asking for carbon-aware routing as a purchase criterion — and the model providers most central to that narrative (OpenAI, Anthropic) are moving toward less transparency, not more, which narrows rather than widens what a gateway feature could even report on.

## Caveats

- This report's method departs from this directory's own adversarial multi-agent-vote protocol (used in `gateway-2026-09-06.md` and others) — it is single-pass direct primary-source verification instead. Two claims in particular rest on exactly one primary source each rather than being cross-checked against a second independent one: LowRouter's own self-description (no independent third-party coverage of it was found), and the two very recent (Aug/Sept 2026) academic carbon-routing papers (`arXiv:2608.06188`, `arXiv:2609.06784`) — both single-paper, not-yet-independently-replicated results, and the first of those two explicitly reports a large gap between its own live-observed effect (1.45%) and its modeled-potential upper bound (50.9%), which this report has deliberately not rounded up to "carbon-aware routing saves ~50%" anywhere above.
- AWS Bedrock's *model-specific* (as opposed to account-level) carbon disclosure was not found in this pass — this is an absence-of-evidence finding, not a confirmed "AWS discloses nothing per-model" claim; Bedrock also hosts third-party models (Anthropic, Meta, etc.) whose own disclosure posture would need checking separately from AWS's own infrastructure-level tooling.
- Google's 2025 Environmental Report (`sustainability.google/reports/google-2025-environmental-report/`) was confirmed to exist and cover FY2024 energy/AI/resilience data, but this pass did not extract Google's own aggregate emissions-growth percentage from it (unlike Microsoft's, which was directly quoted) — deliberately not stated above to avoid presenting a memorized-but-unverified figure as freshly checked; see Open Questions.
- This report's initial WebFetch sanity-check (against Google's own inference-footprint blog post and the Green Software Foundation's SDK repo) returned specific, internally consistent, independently-datable content rather than generic or suspiciously uniform filler, which is the basis for trusting the rest of this pass's fetched content — but this is a spot-check on two sources, not a guarantee extended to every one of the ~25 sources cited above.
- "Real published carbon-footprint data for LLM inference by provider/region," as asked in the research brief, exists in meaningfully rigorous form for exactly one provider (Google) and in third-party-estimated form (not provider-disclosed) for the rest — this asymmetry, not a lack of any data at all, is the accurate characterization. Even Google's own disclosure should be read with the market-based-accounting and self-audit caveats now folded into Finding 2 above — those were not in this report's first-pass draft and were added after direct verification.
- Sasha Luccioni, Boris Gamazaychikov, Theo Alves da Costa, and Emma Strubell's "Misinformation by Omission: The Need for More Environmental Transparency in AI" (`arXiv:2506.15572`, submitted 2025-06-18) is directly on-theme for this report's own "be skeptical of vague green-AI claims" mandate — Google's own Aug 2025 paper cites it — but on direct fetch, its abstract argues the general, industry-wide case that declining transparency has let emissions "misinformation" spread, and does not itself name Google or critique Google's specific market-based-accounting methodology (which postdates it by two months). The accurate framing is that Google's paper is arguably an unusually transparent response to the pattern Luccioni et al. describe, not a target of it — this report did not find a direct rebuttal of Google's own Aug 2025 figures from Luccioni or anyone else.
- The Green Software Foundation's Carbon Aware SDK (Finding 3) was independently re-confirmed active (not archived or superseded) via a second, separate check of its GitHub repository beyond the initial sanity-check fetch — consistent commit/contributor activity, no archive notice. Its own README separately discloses a real, self-acknowledged methodology gap worth noting: it currently standardizes only average location-based carbon-intensity data (gCO2/kWh) and does not yet compute or expose energy-mix (renewable/fossil breakdown) or marginal-emissions data, flagging energy-mix as a possible future feature rather than a current capability — the same average-vs-marginal distinction that the "cleanest grid" paper cited in the first caveat above (`arXiv:2608.06188`) uses WattTime's *marginal* operating emissions rate specifically to avoid.
