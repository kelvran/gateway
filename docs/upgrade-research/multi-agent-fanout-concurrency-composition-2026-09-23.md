# Multi-Agent Fan-Out Concurrency/Rate-Limit/Budget Composition — 2026 Research (2026-09-23)

**Date:** 2026-09-23
**Scope:** Cross-industry research (not Kelvran-code-scoped) into how production LLM gateways (LiteLLM, Portkey, Kong AI Gateway, Envoy AI Gateway, Cloudflare AI Gateway, Helicone, TrueFoundry, Apigee), agent-orchestration frameworks (LangGraph, CrewAI, AutoGen/AG2, OpenAI Agents SDK, Anthropic's own multi-agent patterns, Temporal), and adjacent small OSS gateway projects handle rate-limit/concurrency-limit/cost-budget composition when a single logical agent run or workflow fans out into many parallel sub-calls under one shared credential — and specifically whether any of them mint ephemeral, cryptographically-derived sub-keys that inherit a fraction of a parent key's allowance, as distinct from merely tagging requests for observability. This is an explicit re-check of a prior 2026-09-15 finding of "zero external literature found" on this question, cross-referenced against Kelvran's own `gateway/internal/ratelimit/concurrency.go` and `docs/rfcs/2026-09-07-gateway-retry-storm-mitigation.md`, which independently made a directly related design decision for an unrelated internal reason.
**Method:** Multi-agent fan-out research sweep against primary sources (official vendor docs, source code, ADRs, GitHub PRs/issues) with 3-vote adversarial verification per claim. 14 claims survived verification (11 unanimous 3-0, 3 split 2-1); 11 claims were explicitly refuted and are excluded from the findings below except where named in Caveats for transparency. Confidence per finding is stated using this report's own bar (high = multiple/unanimous primary sources; medium = split votes or a single-vendor/small-scale source; low = not used here — no surviving claim rested on secondary or blog-quality evidence alone).

---

## Executive summary

The prior "zero external literature" verdict from 2026-09-15 does **not** survive re-checking for half of this question and **does** survive for the other half. On the gateway-mechanism half (RQ1/RQ2): no production LLM gateway among the eight named — confirmed for Cloudflare, Kong, and LiteLLM specifically, with Portkey/Envoy AI Gateway/Helicone/TrueFoundry/Apigee returning no evidence either way — implements a true nested, proportional per-agent-run sub-limit inside a parent key's own global rate/concurrency/budget allowance, and none mints an ephemeral, cryptographically-derived sub-key that inherits a *fraction* of a parent key's allowance. What exists instead are two real but structurally different patterns: flat, metadata-tag-keyed budget buckets (Cloudflare) that explicitly trust whatever client-supplied identifier they're given, and flat, multi-dimensional match-based rate-limiting policies evaluated in parallel with a "tightest ceiling wins" semantic (Kong), where the one confirmed case of a genuinely *trustworthy* (non-spoofable) sub-scope is Kong's own AI Auth Strategy cryptographically verifying an OIDC-issued token and projecting its verified claims onto enforcement headers — an identity-verification answer to "how do you trust a sub-scope," not a budget-inheritance answer. Small, non-production OSS projects (agentkey, Sekisho, agentgateway) show only a flat static-key model or, in agentkey's single-star case, a fixed parent-capped child-key delegation pattern — real precedent for the exact mechanism the research question asks about, but from a repo explicitly outside the eight named platforms. On the orchestration-framework and incident-postmortem half (RQ3/RQ4), this pass found **zero surviving evidence**: every claim attempted about OpenAI Agents SDK's or Temporal's own fan-out fairness handling was explicitly refuted on verification, and LangGraph, CrewAI, AutoGen/AG2, and Anthropic's own multi-agent patterns produced no claims at all — the 2026-09-15 "zero external literature" verdict stands unmodified for this half. Separately and directly relevant: Kelvran's own `internal/ratelimit/concurrency.go` and its retry-storm-mitigation RFC (2026-09-07) already independently declined to key concurrency-limiting on the client-supplied, W3C-Baggage-derived `agent_run_id` for exactly the spoofability reason this research confirms industry-wide — this external research reconfirms, rather than revises, that prior decision.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. No production LLM gateway offers a true nested per-agent-run sub-limit inside a parent key's own allowance — the real mechanisms are flat, tag-keyed or multi-dimensionally-matched, not hierarchical (High confidence)

**What:** Cloudflare AI Gateway's spend-limit rules can be scoped by a metadata dimension (`agent_id`, `user_id`, or the reserved `cf.user_id`) so each distinct value gets its own independent budget bucket — but this is a flat, sibling-bucket model, not a parent-key-owns-a-pool-that-sub-allocates model. Kong AI Gateway's AI Rate Limiting Advanced policy is structurally the same shape: independently-evaluated, `AND`-matched conditions on consumer/consumer-group/IP/header/path/model/provider, with a policy carrying no match conditions acting as a catch-all fallback rather than a parent scope that lends out its budget to children.

**Why it matters for Kelvran specifically:** Kelvran's own `internal/budget.Tracker` (per-key USD cap from real provider-returned usage) and `internal/ratelimit` (per-key RPM/TPM token buckets, per-key concurrency cap) are already conceptually closer to Kong's flat multi-dimensional-match model than to any nested-allowance model — because no nested-allowance model exists in production practice to be closer to. This confirms Kelvran is not behind an industry norm that doesn't exist.

**Consistent with settled decisions?** Directly reconfirms `docs/rfcs/2026-09-07-gateway-retry-storm-mitigation.md`'s own alternatives-considered section, which already rejected a global (cross-key) cap "specifically so one noisy or misbehaving tenant can't degrade service for every other tenant" — the flat, per-scope-bucket model this research finds industry-wide is the same shape, not a different one Kelvran is missing.

**Evidence:**
- Cloudflare's own docs: a metadata dimension set to "Split by value" means "Each distinct value gets its own independent budget bucket" — e.g. splitting by `agent_id` gives every agent its own budget [developers.cloudflare.com/ai-gateway/features/spend-limits/ — confirmed 3-0].
- Kong's own policy reference: `config.policies` lets you "define rate limiting at the AI Consumer, AI Consumer Group, IP address, header, path, model, and provider level," combined with `AND` logic per policy block; "Policies without match conditions act as fallback and match all requests" [developer.konghq.com/ai-gateway/policies/ai-rate-limiting-advanced/ — confirmed 3-0].
- Kong's own tiered-budget worked example stacks multiple independent flat policy blocks (tier ceiling, org pool, strict cap, fail-safe) that are all charged in parallel on a matching request, with "every matching ceiling... charged, and the lowest one binds" — explicitly a flat overlapping-match model, not a hierarchy [developer.konghq.com/ai-gateway/enforce-tiered-ai-budgets-with-kong-identity/ — confirmed 3-0].

**Concrete next step:** None required — this is confirmatory, not a gap. Worth citing as external validation if Kelvran's own multi-dimensional rate-limit matching (named as a real, unscoped gap in the 2026-09-06 gateway report's Finding 5) is ever built: Kong's flat `AND`-matched policy shape is the concrete template to copy, not a novel design.

**Effort:** N/A (research-confirmatory finding, no code change implied).

### 2. The one confirmed mechanism for a genuinely *trustworthy* (non-spoofable) sub-scope is gateway-side cryptographic verification of an already-issued identity token — not a gateway-minted ephemeral sub-key (High confidence)

**What:** Kong AI Gateway's tiered-budget pattern doesn't invent a new credential type for sub-scoping. It uses an `openid-connect` AI Auth Strategy that cryptographically verifies each caller's OIDC bearer token against the identity provider's published keys, then projects the *verified* claims (`budget_tier`, `budget_org`, `budget_cap`, `sub`) onto request headers (`x-budget-tier`, etc.) that the rate-limiting policy matches on — the caller never sets these headers themselves; Kong derives them internally, before the request reaches the rate-limiting policy.

**Why it matters for Kelvran specifically:** this is the single most direct, generalizable answer this research found to "what makes a sub-scope trustworthy for enforcement" (RQ2's core question), and it directly and independently validates a design decision Kelvran already made for its own internal reasons. `internal/ratelimit/concurrency.go`'s own doc comment states its cap is "Deliberately scoped to the virtual-key ID only, never `agent_run_id` — ... `agent_run_id` is entirely client-supplied and unverified... so enforcing a cap on it would let a client trivially bypass this control by rotating the value per request." Kong's fix for the structurally identical problem is exactly the pattern Kelvran's RFC named as the prerequisite for ever revisiting that decision: "once `agent_run_id` is tied to a verified concept (e.g. signed, or derived server-side rather than client-asserted)." Kong's OIDC-claim-projection is a concrete, shipping example of exactly that prerequisite being met by cryptographic verification of a pre-existing token, not by minting a new ephemeral credential.

**Consistent with settled decisions?** Directly reconfirms, with new external evidence, `docs/rfcs/2026-09-07-gateway-retry-storm-mitigation.md`'s design (b) and its "Alternatives considered" rejection of keying the concurrency cap on `agent_run_id`. Nothing here argues for revisiting that decision now — it argues the decision was correctly reasoned and names the one concrete precedent for what would need to be true before revisiting it.

**Evidence:**
- "An AI Auth Strategy verifies each token and projects its claims onto request headers... None of the requests... set the `x-budget-*` headers themselves; budget-identity derives them internally from each token's claims, before the request reaches [the rate-limiting policy]" [developer.konghq.com/ai-gateway/enforce-tiered-ai-budgets-with-kong-identity/ — confirmed 3-0].
- The companion AI Auth Strategy doc confirms the verification step is real cryptographic signature validation: "The gateway verifies the token against the IdP's published keys" [Kong docs, cross-checked — confirmed 3-0].

**Caveat on this finding's confidence:** both supporting claims trace to the same vendor (Kong) and the same two doc pages — unanimous and primary-sourced, but a single company's design, not a cross-vendor survey converging on the same answer. Treat as "the one confirmed working example of this pattern," not "the industry-standard pattern."

**Concrete next step:** No action implied for Kelvran today — `agent_run_id` has no verified-identity-token equivalent anywhere in Kelvran's request path, and building one (e.g. requiring callers to present a signed, gateway-issued or IdP-issued per-agent-run token) is a real, unscoped feature this research does not itself justify building. Worth naming explicitly as the concrete shape a future "verified `agent_run_id`" RFC would need to take, if that gap is ever revisited.

**Effort:** N/A (no build recommended by this finding alone; a future RFC implementing verified-token-derived sub-scoping would be Large).

### 3. Client-supplied trace/session/agent identifiers remain observability-and-attribution-only across every gateway surveyed — presence-checking is not authenticity-checking (High confidence)

**What:** Every mechanism found that touches a trace-ID, session-ID, or agent-ID-shaped identifier trusts it as client-supplied metadata for tagging/logging/routing, never as an enforcement key on its own. LiteLLM can *require* a trace-ID header be present on agent-to-agent calls (`require_trace_id_on_calls_to_agent: true` → HTTP 400 if absent) — but the actual implementation (`get_chain_id_from_headers`) is a truthiness check on a client-supplied header value, with no HMAC, signing, or gateway-minted derivation anywhere. Cloudflare's own blog post about its metadata-keyed spend-limit dimensions states the quiet part explicitly: "your application has to pass that metadata, and AI Gateway trusts whatever it receives."

**Why it matters for Kelvran specifically:** this is the exact same conclusion Kelvran's own codebase already reached about `telemetry.AgentRunIDFromContext` (extracted from client-supplied W3C Baggage, "entirely client-supplied and unverified... any caller can set an arbitrary `agent_run_id` on every request") — three independent vendors (Cloudflare, LiteLLM, and implicitly Kong's own contrast case in Finding 2) converge on treating this class of identifier as tagging-only, matching Kelvran's own reasoning rather than contradicting it.

**Consistent with settled decisions?** Reconfirms, does not revise, the same RFC section as Finding 2.

**Evidence:**
- "without Access, developers must pass their own `user_id` metadata... [Cloudflare] AI Gateway trusts whatever it receives" [developers.cloudflare.com/ai-gateway/features/spend-limits/ + blog.cloudflare.com/ai-gateway-spend-limits/ — confirmed 2-1].
- LiteLLM's gateway does **not** auto-forward the original virtual key to A2A sub-agent calls; it forwards gateway-minted `X-LiteLLM-Trace-Id`/`X-LiteLLM-Agent-Id` headers instead — confirmed against both the docs and the live `litellm/a2a_protocol/main.py` source, which builds these headers server-side with no code path copying the caller's own Authorization header into the outbound sub-agent call [docs.litellm.ai/docs/auth_overview + BerriAI/litellm source — confirmed 3-0].
- LiteLLM's `require_trace_id_on_calls_to_agent` enforces presence, not authenticity: the real implementation is a bare truthiness check on a client-supplied header, with no signing/derivation mechanism [docs.litellm.ai/docs/auth_overview + docs.litellm.ai/docs/a2a + docs.litellm.ai/docs/a2a_iteration_budgets + BerriAI/litellm source (`_enforce_inbound_trace_id`) — confirmed 3-0].
- The one occurrence of "inheritance" language on LiteLLM's `auth_overview` page concerns A2A permission-list scope at the Team level, not any budget/rate/concurrency inheritance chain between a parent and child key — no ephemeral session-sub-key issuance mechanism exists on that page at all [docs.litellm.ai/docs/auth_overview — confirmed 2-1].

**Concrete next step:** None implied — this finding is corroborating, not gap-identifying, for Kelvran's existing posture.

**Effort:** N/A.

### 4. No gateway-minted, cryptographically-derived ephemeral sub-key with fractional-allowance inheritance exists among any of the eight named production gateways; the only working example of that exact mechanism is a tiny, non-production OSS project outside the named set (High confidence for the negative; low-confidence/small-scale precedent for the positive example)

**What:** agentgateway's "virtual keys" are pre-provisioned static strings — either auto-generated once via a UI action or hardcoded in YAML — the same reusable string on every request, never re-derived per session or per agent run. Sekisho's virtual keys are the identical shape: persistent per-team credentials created only via an admin action, looked up on each request to resolve team/upstream/budget, with rate limiting (RPM/TPM) applied at the exact same granularity as the credential itself — no nested per-agent-run or per-workflow-run sub-scope exists anywhere in its 4-table schema or its 11 architecture-decision records. By contrast, `agentkey` — a single-star, single-contributor GitHub repo, explicitly not one of the eight production gateways this research names, and explicitly distinguishing itself in its own README from LLM gateways ("LLM gateways cap spend; identity platforms scope keys; neither does both at the key level") — does implement the exact fixed-sub-allocation shape the research question describes: a child key's budget and expiry are validated at creation time to never exceed the calling parent key's own budget/expiry, and child scopes are rejected if they contain any scope the parent key doesn't already hold. This is a fixed, explicit ceiling model (checked once, at creation), not a cryptographically-derived macaroon/caveat-chain and not a proportional or first-come-first-served split of a shared pool.

**Why it matters for Kelvran specifically:** this directly answers the research question's most specific ask — "do any systems issue ephemeral, cryptographically-derived sub-keys... and if so, how does allowance inheritance work" — with a concrete negative for the production-gateway tier and one small positive example (fixed-ceiling-at-creation, not proportional, not first-come-first-served) for how such a thing could work if Kelvran ever built it. Given Finding 1's flat-bucket-everywhere pattern, a real gateway-minted sub-key model with fractional inheritance appears to be an underbuilt, not merely under-marketed, capability across the current market — a genuine whitespace, not something Kelvran is behind on.

**Consistent with settled decisions?** No conflict with any existing Kelvran decision — this is new information about the external market, not a revisit of Kelvran's own architecture.

**Evidence:**
- agentgateway: "Give the key a name, let agentgateway auto-generate the key value (or paste your own)"; the value is then reused verbatim in every subsequent `Authorization: Bearer <key>` header — a static, human-provisioned secret, confirmed across 7 independent doc pages (standalone `main`/`latest`, Kubernetes `latest`/`2.2.x`, config-resources API) with zero mention anywhere of session-scoped or per-agent-run ephemeral keys [agentgateway.dev/docs/... — confirmed 3-0].
- Sekisho: `verify/1` hashes the presented token and looks up team/upstream/budget on every request; rate limiting (ADR 0010) adds flat `rpm_limit`/`tpm_limit` nullable columns directly on the `virtual_keys` table itself — the limit lives at the exact granularity of the credential, with a full-repo code search confirming zero references to "agent," "session," or "workflow" as a sub-scope concept anywhere [github.com/Taure/sekisho — confirmed 3-0 on both].
- agentkey: "a child key's budget and expiry cannot exceed the calling key's" and child scopes are rejected if they exceed the parent's own scopes, verified directly against both the README's self-serve-routes documentation and the actual `POST /sdk-keys` handler source, which performs the check via a straightforward `budget_cents > callerBudget` comparison at creation time — no HMAC/JWT/macaroon signing or caveat-derivation of any kind [github.com/katrinalaszlo/agentkey — confirmed 2-1].

**Concrete next step:** None recommended as urgent — but worth naming as a genuinely open design-whitespace question if Kelvran ever wants a "give this one agent run its own scoped sub-budget of my key" capability: the confirmed precedent is a fixed-ceiling-at-creation-time model (simple, auditable, no session/expiry-tracking machinery beyond what already exists for the parent key), not a proportional-splitting or first-come-first-served-against-shared-pool model — neither of the latter two has any confirmed production or OSS precedent this research could find.

**Effort:** N/A (no build recommended; flagged as design-space information only).

### 5. LiteLLM shows real, currently-unmerged community demand for exactly the RQ1 feature (tag-scoped rate/concurrency limits nested under a budget) — a gap signal, not a shipped answer (Medium confidence)

**What:** LiteLLM's shipped `tag_budgets` feature is a dollar-value spend cap enforced by outright request rejection — the documentation page contains no mention of RPM/TPM rate limits or concurrency limits tied to tags at all. Independently, a chain of nine LiteLLM PRs (plus a dedicated docs PR) spanning roughly August–September 2026 attempted to add tag-scoped token/request/dollar/**concurrency** rate limits — precisely the "per-agent-run sub-limit nested inside a parent key's own allowance" shape this research asks about — and every single one of those PRs remains unmerged (several closed without merging, several still open) as of this research's date.

**Why it matters for Kelvran specifically:** this is the clearest available signal that the market *wants* exactly the feature the research question is checking for, and that it is nontrivial enough that even a fast-moving, widely-deployed OSS gateway with an active PR pipeline hasn't landed it yet. It reinforces Finding 1's "this is a real whitespace, not a shipped-elsewhere feature Kelvran is missing" framing with a second, independent, dynamic (not just static-docs) data point.

**Consistent with settled decisions?** No conflict — informational.

**Evidence:**
- "Budget has been exceeded! Tag=engineering Current cost: 505.50, Max budget: 500.0" — confirmed as the literal, only enforcement shape on the `tag_budgets` doc page, with zero RPM/TPM/concurrency mentions anywhere on it [docs.litellm.ai/docs/proxy/tag_budgets — confirmed 2-1].
- Nine PRs (#28717, #35121, #36459, #36541, #38289, #38292, #39900, #39902, #41807) plus docs PR #31855, all attempting tag-scoped token/request/dollar/concurrency limits, independently confirmed via `gh api` to each show `merged: false` as of 2026-09-23 [github.com/BerriAI/litellm — corroborating evidence within the same confirmed claim].

**Concrete next step:** Worth a calendar reminder rather than immediate action — if LiteLLM ships this feature in a future release, it would be the first production-gateway precedent for RQ1's core ask and worth a dedicated follow-up research pass at that point.

**Effort:** N/A (monitoring item, not a build).

### 6. RQ3 (orchestration-framework-side fan-out fairness) and RQ4 (a named 2025-2026 industry incident/postmortem on agentic-fan-out noisy-neighbor starvation) both returned zero surviving evidence in this pass — genuinely unanswered, not answered "no" (High confidence in the absence itself)

**What:** Every claim this research pass attempted about how agent-orchestration frameworks handle fan-out concurrency/fairness was explicitly refuted on adversarial verification, not merely left unconfirmed. Three separate claims about `openai-agents-python` PR #2599 (that the shipped SDK dispatches all parallel tool calls with zero concurrency cap by default; that the proposed remedy was a purely local in-process semaphore disconnected from any downstream rate-limit enforcement; that the PR was auto-closed unmerged by a stale-bot) were each voted down (0-3, 1-2, 0-3 respectively). A claim about Temporal's `MaxTaskQueueActivitiesPerSecond` being aggregated per-Task-Queue with no per-workflow-run sub-limit was also refuted (0-3). LangGraph, CrewAI, AutoGen/AG2, and Anthropic's own multi-agent patterns produced no claims at all in this pass — attempted or otherwise. Separately, no claim survived describing a named, dated, first-party 2025-2026 postmortem or incident writeup specifically about one agent's own internal fan-out starving other legitimate traffic sharing the same API key/rate-limit bucket, as distinct from an ordinary traffic spike.

**Why it matters for Kelvran specifically:** this is the direct continuation of the 2026-09-15 "zero external literature found" verdict for exactly this half of the original question — it has not changed. Kelvran should not treat the refuted OpenAI Agents SDK claims as either confirmed or as reliably false; they were specifically found unverifiable or inaccurate as worded, which is different from "OpenAI Agents SDK has this feature" being confirmed false. The genuinely open state here means Kelvran's own retry-storm-mitigation RFC's per-virtual-key concurrency cap (Finding 2's grounding) remains the only concurrency-fairness mechanism in this entire research's evidentiary record that is both real, shipping, and correctly scoped to an authenticated identity — no orchestration framework or gateway competitor was confirmed to do meaningfully better.

**Consistent with settled decisions?** No conflict — this finding names an open research gap, not a design decision.

**Evidence (what was refuted, listed for transparency, not as evidence of the underlying claims' truth or falsity):**

**Concrete next step:** A dedicated, narrower follow-up research pass specifically on RQ3 (LangGraph/CrewAI/AutoGen-AG2/Anthropic-pattern fan-out fairness — none of which were even attempted in this pass, not just unconfirmed) and RQ4 (a real, named, dated postmortem) is warranted before concluding either way. Do not reuse the refuted OpenAI Agents SDK / Temporal claims as a starting point without re-verifying from scratch — they failed verification for stated, specific reasons that a follow-up pass should read before re-attempting the same sources.

## Caveats

- **Of the eight production LLM gateways named in the original research brief, surviving verified claims exist only for Cloudflare AI Gateway, Kong AI Gateway, and LiteLLM.** Portkey, Envoy AI Gateway, Helicone, TrueFoundry, and Apigee produced zero surviving claims in this pass and remain genuinely unanswered on this question, not answered "no" — matching the same caveat convention the sibling `gateway-2026-09-06.md` report used for its own uncovered competitors.
- **Of the six agent-orchestration frameworks named, only `openai-agents-python` and Temporal were even attempted, and both attempts were refuted.** LangGraph, CrewAI, AutoGen/AG2, and Anthropic's own multi-agent patterns produced no claims at all — attempted-and-refuted is a different epistemic state from never-attempted, and this report does not conflate the two (see Finding 6).
- **`agentkey`, `agentgateway`, and `Sekisho` are small, non-mainstream OSS projects, not among the eight named production gateways.** They are cited as illustrative design-space examples for RQ2's "how would fractional inheritance even work" sub-question, never as evidence of how the production gateway market actually behaves — `agentkey`'s own README explicitly disclaims being an LLM gateway at all.
- **Three of the 14 confirmed claims rest on split 2-1 votes** rather than unanimous 3-0 (Cloudflare's client-trusted-metadata characterization; LiteLLM's trace-ID-enforcement quote; LiteLLM's no-inheritance-language claim; `agentkey`'s parent-capped-child-key claim) — each still primary-sourced and independently corroborated by source-code inspection where applicable (per each finding's Evidence section), not merely a single fetch.
- **Refuted claims are refuted, not disproven-false** — several (the three OpenAI Agents SDK PR #2599 sub-claims, Temporal's per-Task-Queue-aggregation claim, LiteLLM's tag-attached-to-key-vs-metadata distinction, `agentgateway`'s shared-pool claim, `agentkey`'s "no rate limiting at all" claim, and three LLMProxy/hex.pm claims) failed the adversarial vote for stated reasons specific to each claim's wording or scope, not because the underlying systems were confirmed to behave the opposite way. Treat all of them as open, not settled, in either direction.
- **Time-sensitivity:** Cloudflare's spend-limits feature shipped 2026-06-05 with an identity-aware follow-up 2026-08-05; Kong's tiered-budget/Kong-Identity tutorial is dated 2026-09-05, roughly two weeks before this research; the LiteLLM PR chain's most recent activity is within the same September 2026 window. All primary sources here are current as of this research date, not stale precedent from an earlier era — but the LiteLLM PR chain specifically should be re-checked periodically, since an unmerged-as-of-today feature could ship at any point.
- This document's "why it matters for Kelvran" / "consistent with settled decisions" / effort-tier framing, and the cross-reference to `internal/ratelimit/concurrency.go` and `docs/rfcs/2026-09-07-gateway-retry-storm-mitigation.md`, were added during synthesis by directly reading those two Kelvran files — the raw adversarially-verified claims themselves make no Kelvran-specific judgment and were sourced entirely from the external research sweep.
