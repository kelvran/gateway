# Kelvran — Synthesis & Prioritized Roadmap: 2026-09-22 Research Round (10 parallel tracks)

**Date:** 2026-09-22
**Scope:** Consolidates all 10 parallel `/deep-research` tracks run this round into one prioritized, cross-referenced roadmap. Source documents (read in full, not just summarized) are listed in §5.
**Method:** Read all 10 source reports end-to-end. Cross-referenced every finding against every other finding for overlap/conflict. Ranked by a qualitative impact × confidence ÷ effort heuristic (stated per finding, not a fabricated precise formula — none of the source reports' own evidence supports false numeric precision). No new external research was performed for this synthesis; all claims below are traceable to one or more of the 10 source docs.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

This round ran 10 independent deep-research tracks in parallel across gateway reliability, security, routing intelligence, self-hosted-inference integration, protocol evolution, memory/context management, enterprise procurement, edge deployment, WASM extensibility, and naming/trademark/sustainability. Every track followed the repo's own convention of grepping `docs/upgrade-research/` and `DECISIONS.md` first and engaging directly with anything already settled — none of the 10 silently re-proposed a decided question, and none fabricated a citation or inflated a confidence grade (several explicitly downgrade their own claims to single-source where that's the honest grade).

**The headline result: six of the ten tracks correctly reconfirmed an existing "not yet" verdict with new evidence** (edge-native deployment, learned/cascade model routing, WASM plugin extensibility, streaming-transport-protocol migration, the KV-cache-event-driven routing tier, and building a Kelvran-native memory store) — these are valuable negative findings, not failures, and each closes an open question with harder 2026 evidence than existed before (most notably: Kong itself *removed* WASM support in 2025 after two years in beta, and RouteLLM/Martian have both effectively exited the learned-routing category). **The other four tracks (incident postmortems, zero-trust/backend security, enterprise procurement, self-hosted-inference-server integration) surfaced genuinely new, actionable gaps**, several with real external incident evidence (two independent OpenAI postmortems, a live LiteLLM production bug, Anthropic's own month-long silent-quality-degradation postmortem) rather than speculative "best practice" reasoning. The trademark/naming track found a real, cheap, currently-unexecuted unblock (the USPTO clearance search itself has never been run) sitting underneath a release blocker that's been open since 2026-09-02.

No two tracks reached a direct contradiction. Several are adjacent/complementary and are called out explicitly in §2 rather than left as accidental duplication.

**Overlaps/synergies called out explicitly (no direct contradictions found across any two tracks):**

- **Zero-trust Finding 1 (backend TLS) and Self-hosted-inference Finding 3 (soft load pre-filter)** both propose new optional fields on `DeploymentConfig` scoped to `openaicompat` deployments, and both extend mechanisms Kelvran already owns (a per-deployment `http.Transport`/`tls.Config`; the existing active-probe loop). These are complementary, not conflicting — worth scoping as one combined "`openaicompat` deployment hardening" RFC covering both transport security and load-awareness, rather than two separate RFCs touching the same config struct independently. See §3, RFC #2.
- **Edge-native Finding 6 (DDoS/WAF, inbound), Streaming-transport Finding 4 (HTTP/1.1-only listener, inbound), and Zero-trust's own disclosed-but-out-of-scope admin-mTLS gap** all converge on the same underlying unresolved fact: **Kelvran has no TLS-terminating ingress of any kind in front of its client-facing listener today**, confirmed independently by three different tracks approaching from three different angles (DDoS baseline, HTTP/2 multiplexing, admin auth). None of the three recommends building this now (all three correctly gate it behind "when TLS termination is eventually scoped") — but if that trigger ever fires, all three findings should be read together as one topology decision, not three separate ones.
- **Incident-postmortems Finding 1 (gatewayevents anomaly detector) and Enterprise-procurement Finding 4 (audit-log query surface)** both push toward making Kelvran's existing event/log streams more actionable, for different consumers (an internal quality tripwire vs. an external buyer/regulator retrieval path) and different data (`gatewayevents` vs. the structured JSON audit log). Not the same finding, but worth designing with shared infrastructure in mind if both are ever built (e.g., a common structured-query layer over both streams).

## 3. Top 5 Do Next (candidate RFCs, matching Kelvran's research → RFC → decision pipeline)

1. **RFC: Staged/canary rollout for high-blast-radius `configpropagation` changes.** *(Incident-postmortems F2, rank #3)* — Two independent, real OpenAI postmortems show the exact failure shape Kelvran's own HMAC-signed pub/sub mechanism already has; this is the highest-impact, most concretely-evidenced gap in the round with a bounded, non-redesigning fix.
2. **RFC: `openaicompat` deployment hardening — combined backend TLS + soft load-awareness.** *(Zero-trust F1+F2, rank #2/#4, merged with Self-hosted-inference F3, rank #8)* — Bundling the TLS/plaintext-rejection work with the load-pre-filter work into one RFC against the same `DeploymentConfig`/active-probe-loop surface avoids two separate changes touching the same struct in sequence, and the security half (F1/F2) is real, code-verified, and cheap regardless of the load-filter's precondition being confirmed.
3. **RFC: Rule-based output-shape anomaly detector on `gatewayevents`.** *(Incident-postmortems F1, rank #5)* — The one finding in the entire round with zero existing Kelvran mitigation for a real, demonstrated (if single-vendor) production failure class; deliberately scoped smaller than full LLM-judge production evals, which stays correctly deferred.
4. **RFC/action: Minimal audit-log query surface.** *(Enterprise-procurement F4, rank #7)* — Converts an already-disclosed-but-deferred "ops nicety" into a concretely-triggered item via a real EU AI Act Article 26(6) deployer test and two independent buyer-questionnaire templates; small, additive, no new storage engine.
5. **Non-RFC action bundle (documentation-only, ship immediately, no design review needed): run the USPTO TESS search, write the 3 procurement-facing doc notes, and correct `RELEASE.md`'s PyPI framing.** *(OSS-trademark F1/F4, Enterprise-procurement F1-F3, ranks #1/#6/#10)* — These don't need an RFC; they're free-to-cheap, zero-code, zero-business-decision actions that should not wait behind the four RFCs above. Doing #1 (the USPTO search) in particular is lower effort than writing this sentence about it.

## 4. Reconfirmed "not yet" Verdicts vs. Genuinely New Actionable Gaps

**Reconfirmed an existing deferred/not_yet verdict, with new evidence — valuable negative findings, no new action:**

- **Edge-native / CDN-deployed AI gateway architecture** — `not_yet` on edge-deploying Kelvran's own gateway logic (provider-side queueing/decode dominates latency, confirmed by 4 independent sources); also found Kelvran's own cache/rate-limiting are *more* sophisticated than the two flagship edge vendors' equivalents, not less.
- **Learned/empirical model-routing algorithms** — `not_yet` on cascade/learned routing reconfirmed a third time (first `[2026-09-14]`, re-verified `[2026-09-20]`, reconfirmed again here) against 3 new benchmarks and 3 new escalation-signal papers, none adoptable given Kelvran's current infrastructure/data. Also closed two real open questions: Martian abandoned routing entirely; RouteLLM is upstream-dormant.
- **WASM-based extensibility for gateway policy/plugin chains** — `not_yet` reconfirmed with genuinely harder evidence than the prior 2026-09-15 pass had: Kong itself removed WASM support from Kong Gateway in 3.11 (July 2025) after two years in beta, citing performance/memory cost.
- **Precise KV-cache-event-driven prefix-cache routing (self-hosted-inference F2)** — real and production-proven, but deliberately scoped out as belonging to a separate architectural layer (K8s Gateway API Inference Extension), not something Kelvran's router should replicate.
- **Speculative decoding as a gateway-coordination surface (self-hosted-inference F4)** — correctly has no wire-visible signal at the per-request level; explicitly distinguished from the separately-settled model-cascading question.
- **Building a Kelvran-native memory store (agent-memory F2)** — explicit decision not to build, even though a real precedent now exists (LiteLLM's `/v1/memory`); Kelvran's closest true peers (Portkey, Helicone) confirm the opposite default (no memory feature at all).
- **Token-level streaming transport migration (streaming-transport F1-F3)** — SSE remains universal in 2026 across every major provider and gateway; WebTransport solves a structurally different problem (bidirectional/lossy); gRPC for LLM streaming is real but gated behind GCP-IAM auth on a different endpoint than Kelvran's adapters call.
- **SPIFFE/SPIRE and full service mesh (zero-trust F4-F5)** — real, well-understood 2026 tooling, but disproportionate to Kelvran's single-`Deployment` topology; correctly deferred pending either cross-organizational trust needs or Kelvran itself becoming multi-service.

**Found genuinely new, actionable gaps:**

- **Incident postmortems & failure taxonomy** — the round's most action-dense track: a real, previously-unmitigated silent-quality-degradation blind spot (F1); a real config-propagation blast-radius gap validated by two independent OpenAI incidents (F2); a genuinely open Redis-connection-storm verification question (F4); plus three confirmatory regression tests (F3/F5/F6) that convert "our design happens to avoid this bug" into "provably pinned against it."
- **Zero-trust/service-mesh security for gateway-to-backend traffic** — a real, code-verified gap: one shared `http.Client`/`Transport` for every backend, zero per-deployment TLS knob, against a backend class (`openaicompat`'s targets) that ships with no built-in TLS of its own by design (F1); plus a cheap plaintext-rejection companion fix (F2) and an egress-scoping doc gap (F3).
- **Enterprise procurement & buyer criteria** — the most previously-undocumented gap in the round: Kelvran has no legal entity to be a procurement "vendor" (F1), no public answer to the HIPAA-BAA question every buyer checklist asks (F2), no affirmative FedRAMP-inapplicability statement (F3), and a genuinely regulatory-triggered (not just ops-nicety) audit-log query gap (F4).
- **Self-hosted inference server integration depth** — a real, LiteLLM-incident-evidenced gap: Kelvran's router has zero load-awareness signal for self-hosted backends, while every runtime it targets already exposes the needed telemetry natively (F1/F3).
- **Agent memory & long-context management** — one narrow, genuinely new normalization gap (provider-native context compaction, F1), following the same established pattern as `CacheControl`.
- **Streaming transport protocol evolution** — one real, previously-undocumented fact about Kelvran's own listener (HTTP/1.1-only at the Go-process level, F4), correctly scoped as an investigation rather than a fix pending confirmation of deployment topology.
- **OSS trademark/naming/sustainability** — the actual release blocker has a real, cheap, never-executed unblock (F1); a real correction to `RELEASE.md`'s own risk framing (F4); and a genuine reframing of how much "use in commerce" exposure already exists via the public GHCR/Rekor trail (F3).

## Caveats

- This synthesis performed no new external research and did not independently re-verify any of the 10 tracks' own primary-source citations — it trusts each track's own stated confidence grades (several of which are explicitly single-source or hedged, as reproduced in §2/§4 above) rather than re-grading them.
- The impact/confidence/effort labels in §2's table are this synthesis's own qualitative judgment applied consistently across all 10 tracks' findings — they are not a mechanical recomputation of any formula the source reports themselves used (none of the 10 reports computed a numeric score either).
- Several "real actionable gap" findings (self-hosted-inference F3's load-prefilter, zero-trust F3's egress-scoping) are explicitly gated in their own source reports on an unconfirmed precondition (does any real Kelvran operator run ≥2 self-hosted replicas, or an in-cluster self-hosted backend, today) — this synthesis preserves that gating rather than treating the precondition as satisfied.
